Manite Backlash Oracle

Sep 02, 202627 min read

After the ecological disaster that precipitated the downfall of industrialized man, manite continues to play an important role in the economy of Yrt. Mana, which is manite dug up from ecological disaster (“blighted”) zones and refined, is used by the magic community in a variety of ways:

  • Wizards: applied bodily, ingested, or thrown and then funneled into “spells” which are triggered via motion, verbal commands, and concentrated thoughts. The azure layer
    • For example, a fireball spell can be the channeling of all manite to the tip of the fingers and a release of its energy in the form of a jet or ball of flame accompanied by a collapse of the underlying matrix (ash).
    • Side effects of energy release and mana ingestion can include headache, fatigue, and listlessness. Severe side effects include rapid aging, burns, implosion, explosion, mental breakdown or death to those that do not practice singular control.
  • Priests: applied bodily or “burned” into a higher energy state by candles or applied to holy objects. Prayers, mental concentration, movement, and spoken song will trigger the use of manite.

Manite is “mined” by the guilds and temples in blight zones at great cost. Blighted zones have uncontrolled manite outcroppings that manifest themselves in dangerous ways.

Different types of manite are used for different purposes:

  • Gray: the most common type of mana, used for inert objects like construction, furniture, etc.
    • Can assume a variety of textures and colors. Not consumable.
    • 1 to 1 ratio of nanite material and object to be created.
    • Can be used for armor and tactical clothing.
  • Azure (blue): medical, injested or applied topically.
    • Can stitch close wounds, repair organs, muscle tissue, and nerve endings.
    • Will automatically eliminate infection and most common diseases. In skilled hands can resuscitate.
    • Can be used to alter one’s appearance or temporarily boost capabilities (speed, strength)
    • Needs special training to use effectively for more complex tasks.
  • Crimson (red): combat, energy, ingested or used externally.
    • Can be converted to an energy release.
    • Needs special training to use effectively for more complex tasks.
  • Verdant (green): agriculture and plants, applied externally.
    • Promotes growth and pest control.
    • Increases crop yield and the taste of food
  • Luminous (white) – stabilizing, insulating, and warding. • Shapes barriers, hard‑light shields, and wards against radiation, heat, or mind‑effects. • Can “lock” other manite in place (e.g., hold blue changes stable, prevent red from going critical).
  • Amber (yellow) – interfaces tightly with the nervous system and sensory processing. • Projects illusions, dampens fear or pain, or induces specific emotional states. • Highly dangerous regarding addiction, mind‑break, or identity erosion.*
  • Dark (black) – unstable, drawn from the deepest, most corrupted blight zones. • Accelerates decay, leeches heat and life, animates dead tissue or constructs. • Often illegal or heavily regulated; long‑term use causes corruption or feralism‑like effects.

Gray mana can be absorbed directly from the environment during spell casting or can be added to the player’s mana supply through meditation (+1 per hour). Colored mana must be bought from the magic guild while in a town or city that has one.

Mana Seeds: Construction, Shape, and Activation

Mana seeds are the foundation of Conclave spellcraft. Every prepared spell, every measured burst of power, begins as a seed: a carefully shaped cluster of manite, bound into a stable form until the caster’s will calls it awake.

What a Mana Seed Is

A mana seed is a compact, pre‑configured bundle of manite, tuned to a single effect or narrow family of effects.

  • Core: A mix of specialized colors (Gray, Crimson, Azure, Verdant, Luminous, Amber, rarely Black) in precise ratios.
  • Shell: A Gray and Luminous “husk” that holds the structure together, shields it from stray signals, and keeps it inert until triggered.
  • Program: Embedded Amber patterns that define how the seed should unfold: where force flows, what geometry it assumes, which senses it touches, how long it persists.

To the untrained eye, most seeds look like small beads, wafers, or thin etched tokens. To someone with Amber in their skull, each has a distinct “feel”: a weight in the mind’s hand that hints at its potential.

Seed Construction

1. Measuring and Mixing

The first step is selecting and measuring raw manite grains:

  • Color ratios: A basic burning bolt might be mostly Crimson with a thin Gray shell; a warding barrier leans heavily on Luminous and Gray; an illusion seed centers on Amber and Luminous.
  • Grain size: Finer grains respond faster but are harder to stabilize; coarser grains are more robust but slower and less subtle.

Apprentices begin with pre‑weighed packets from the Conclave: small envelopes of “red base,” “white binder,” and “gray shell.” Masters eventually learn to draw directly from bulk supplies and judge by eye and instinct.

2. Shaping the Form

The mix is then shaped into a geometric form that guides how it will deploy:

  • The caster uses simple tools (molds, dies, or Gray workbenches) to press or grow the seed into its intended geometry.
  • During shaping, gentle Amber pulses encourage grains to settle into the correct micro‑structure.
  • Once formed, a Luminous “lash” tightens around the seed, locking it in a dormant state.

Mistakes at this stage lead to seeds that leak, misfire, or collapse when triggered. The best makers talk about “listening” to the seed as they shape it, sensing when the internal flows line up.

3. Encoding the Trigger

Finally, the seed is taught when and how to wake:

  • The caster impresses a short Amber command phrase into the seed, often tied to a word, gesture, or mental image.
  • Safety conditions can be added:
    • Only respond to the maker’s Amber signature.
    • Stay inert if the shell is cracked.
    • Fail safe if exposed to strong corruption.

This is the most jealously guarded part of Conclave lore. Recipes for seeds may be copied, but the exact phrasing and modulation of triggers are often written in code or transmitted only from master to trusted student.

Common Seed Shapes and Uses

Shape is as important as composition. It determines how the seed’s contents spread, where they act, and how quickly they exhaust themselves.

Spheres and Beads

  • Description: Small, dense balls or ovoids.
  • Use: Throwing, wand‑launching, or embedding in devices.
  • Behavior:
    • On activation, burst outward in all directions or project a focused lance along a chosen axis.
    • Ideal for single‑target strikes, flares, and short, sharp barriers.

Cones and Wedges

  • Description: Tapered shapes, thicker at one end, etched or molded to emphasize a direction.
  • Use: Close‑range area spells.
  • Behavior:
    • Unfold in a broad, short cone: burning sprays, shockwaves, sleep fields, fogs.
    • Good for sweeping a doorway, hallway, or small crowd.

Rings and Bands

  • Description: Hollow loops or thin bands, sometimes nested.
  • Use: Wards, circles, barriers.
  • Behavior:
    • Anchor to a point or line and expand into a ring of force, light, or sensory disruption.
    • Common in defensive magic, sanctums, and ritual circles.

Disks and Tiles

  • Description: Flat coins, hexes, or tiles.
  • Use: Underfoot, on walls, inlaid into armor or doors.
  • Behavior:
    • Spread effects across surfaces: friction changes, adhesive fields, traps, glyphs.
    • Useful for pre‑set spells: step here, trigger that.

Threads and Needles

  • Description: Slender rods, pins, or coils.
  • Use: Precision work, surgery, subtle enchantment.
  • Behavior:
    • Deliver narrow, penetrating effects: nerve‑line anesthetics, precise cuts, or focused illusions.
    • Favored by healers, assassins, and illusionists.

Activation and Casting

Even a perfect seed is inert until someone wakes it.

For Trained Casters

An initiated caster with Amber in their brain activates seeds in three linked steps:

  1. Focus: Fix attention on the seed, felt as a small, silent “weight” wherever it’s stored (pouch, bandolier, wand).
  2. Gesture: Trace a pattern with hand, wand, staff, or eye line that tells the seed how to deploy (straight shot, blooming cone, rising wall).
  3. Word or Image: Speak a trigger word or hold a specific mental sigil that matches the Amber key burned into the seed.

When the pattern matches, the seed’s shell slackens and its inner program unfolds. To an outside observer, this looks like instantaneous spellcasting; to the caster, it feels like opening a tightly wound spring in exactly the right direction.

For Non‑Casters

Most seeds are locked: without Amber‑backed focus, they remain inert beads.

The Conclave does sell or issue a limited set of common‑use seeds:

  • One‑step flares: pinch and throw; they burst into light on impact.
  • Panic wards: crush in the hand to raise a momentary, simple barrier.
  • Signal seeds: snap to send a visible or audible pattern.

These are engineered with crude, physical triggers instead of subtle Amber keys, making them simpler but less flexible and more dangerous if mishandled.

What the Unmade Had Instead

The common-use seed is not an invention. It is a substitute, and a poor one, for something the world used to have.

The Veil was never universal, even before the Fall. Taking one on is a procedure and the procedure carries real risk: the connection is made in living tissue, it does not always seat cleanly, and the failures range from a candidate who simply never perceives anything to a candidate who is quietly and permanently altered in ways nobody detects for years. The Unmade understood this at least as well as the Conclave does. They veiled the people whose work required depth: construction engineers, artificers, refiners, physicians, the trades where you must listen to what you are working and hold that listening for hours. It was a qualification, not a birthright, and it was not taken lightly.

Everyone else carried a device. Not a seed and not a wand in the Conclave’s sense: a small personal instrument, held in the hand, that let an ordinary unveiled person work mana directly at a lesser but genuinely useful degree. The expertise lived in the instrument. The user did not need to know what they were doing in any deep sense, any more than a man needs to understand a lock to turn a key.

What such a thing does, on the evidence of the few that have ever been recovered: light on demand and light that lasts. Heat for a pot or a room. Sealing a crack, setting a joint, stiffening a rotten board: small Gray work, at the scale of a household. Clean water. A ward across a doorway that will hold overnight. Simple Azure first aid: closing a cut, damping a fever, setting a small bone badly but well enough. Fire without flint.

What it does not do is anything requiring judgement sustained over time. It will not run a waking trough, because a trough is not a task but a conversation: the call spoken and re-spoken, the answer listened for, the batch read by the drift of the film and the sound of the paddle. It will not do illusion of any subtlety, or compulsion at all, or anything the Vizion order would recognise as work. It is a household tool, and it was built for households.

That distinction is the whole of it, and it is why the pre-Fall world looks impossible from here. The Unmade were not a civilisation of mages. They were a civilisation of ordinary people each holding a small competence, with a thin layer of veiled professionals above them doing the difficult part, which is a description of every craft in every age, and is precisely the arrangement the Fall destroyed.

Why this matters now. The Conclave’s authority does not actually rest on the refining monopoly, which is an accident of capital and geography. It rests on a claim: that mana is dangerous, that working it requires a trained and licensed practitioner, and that the alternative to the order is not freedom but corpses. The common-use seed exists to prove the point: here is what a layman may safely be given, and look how little it is.

A working stock of Unmade handsets would not break the refining monopoly. No device makes a trough operator; that still takes twenty years and a Veil. What it would break is the claim. Every household in Freeport doing its own light, its own heat, its own small mending, without paying the order or asking its permission. That is not an industrial problem. It is the end of the reason anyone obeys.

They are also, on the two examples ever examined, not charged, and nobody now living knows how they were meant to be. Whether they take a sun-charge like everything else, or drew on something the pre-Fall world had and this one does not, is the question anybody holding one has to answer before it is worth anything at all.

Conclave Seed‑Craft Tools

The Conclave profits as much from tools as from raw mana. Seed‑crafters rely on specialized instruments to shape, stabilize, and test their work.

1. Gray Casting Bench

A work surface grown from dense Gray, etched with standard molds and alignment rails.

  • Function:
    • Reconfigures into different seed molds (sphere, cone, ring, tile) at a thought.
    • Holds grains in precise volumes while the caster adds energy and commands.
  • Benefit:
    • Reduces minor fabrication errors, lets apprentices produce passable work under supervision.

2. Luminous Clamp

A set of adjustable rings or arms that project thin Luminous fields around a forming seed.

  • Function:
    • Provides temporary containment while internal structure settles.
    • Shields the seed from stray Amber signals and ambient fields.
  • Benefit:
    • Greatly lowers the chance of premature activation or slow leakage.
    • Essential for high‑energy Crimson seeds and delicate illusion seeds.

3. Amber Tuning Wand

A slim rod or stylus, heavily laced with Amber, that resonates with a caster’s implanted Amber layer.

  • Function:
    • Lets the caster “write” precise trigger patterns into seeds.
    • Amplifies subtle intent, smoothing over tremors or lapses in concentration.
  • Benefit:
    • Shortens training time for initiates.
    • Allows older or injured mages to continue precise work.

4. Grain Sieve and Sorter

A box or frame containing layers of Gray filters and micro‑lattices.

  • Function:
    • Sorts raw manite feedstock by grain size and color class.
    • Can separate out contaminated or partially corrupted grains.
  • Benefit:
    • Ensures consistent performance across batches.
    • Reduces the risk that one bad grain will destabilize an entire seed.

5. Calibrated Seed Molds

Portable molds of varying shapes and volumes, often carried by traveling mages.

  • Function:
    • Provide physical reference shapes for standard spells.
    • Some include etched guides for common Amber channels.
  • Benefit:
    • Let casters fabricate or repair basic seeds in the field with minimal equipment.
    • Help apprentices keep “on pattern” until they internalize the geometries.

6. Crimson Pulse Meter

A hand‑held gauge that samples a seed’s stored energy and expected discharge profile.

  • Function:
    • Reads how much usable Crimson (or other active color) is bound in a seed.
    • Flags over‑charged or dangerously unstable seeds.
  • Benefit:
    • Prevents accidental overkill or premature burnout.
    • Allows fine‑tuning of seeds for subtle, low‑signature effects.

7. Seed Ledger and Cipher Deck

Less a physical tool than a working grimoire.

  • Function:
    • Records recipes, shapes, and trigger phrases for each seed design.
    • Uses layered ciphers, mnemonic diagrams, and symbolic shorthand.
  • Benefit:
    • Protects trade secrets and complicates theft: a stolen ledger is nearly useless without the right training and cipher keys.
    • Serves as a focus aid when preparing complex batches.

8. Apprentice’s Harness

A bandolier or belt system with standardized seed slots and safety locks.

  • Function:
    • Holds seeds in known orientations and positions for fast, blind retrieval.
    • Includes simple dampers to prevent jostling from triggering sensitive designs.
  • Benefit:
    • Speeds casting in stressful situations.
    • Reduces the risk of dropping or mis‑grabbing a potent seed.

The Mana Economy

Because seeds consume real manite and depend on Conclave tooling:

  • Costly Training: Learning to make reliable seeds requires access to benches, clamps, meters, and high‑grade feedstock, resources controlled by the Conclave.
  • Ongoing Dependence: Even seasoned mages depend on Conclave supply lines for fresh manite, replacement tools, and updated recipes.
  • Controlled Power: By licensing tools, inspecting workshops, and tracking bulk manite, the Conclave can decide who practices true seed‑craft and who remains a mere user of common seeds.

In practice, a magic‑user’s strength is not only their talent for casting, but also their skill and resources for preparing mana seeds, each bead, cone, and ring a tiny, hand‑built engine of will.

Conclave Ritual: Compulsion

Ritual

This ritual lets a caster influence thoughts, emotions, and perceptions in a target’s mind. It is more dangerous than simple illusions, because it acts inside the mind rather than in the air. Amber mana interfaces tightly with the nervous system and sensory processing. It can project illusions, dampen fear or pain, or induce specific emotional states; extended or forceful use risks addiction, mindbreak, or erosion of identity.

Mana: Amber is required for all compulsion. Luminous (optional) stabilizes the effect and reduces bleed-over. Azure (optional) can soothe physical stress as part of the compulsion, at the risk of deepening dependency. At least 1 mana must be spent.

Risks: Repeated use on the same mind within a short period increases Resistance by +1 each time (max 2) and may create fragmented or contradictory memories. If you roll a miss on a compulsion with Depth 2 or Scope 2, mark a debility related to mental strain (sleeplessness, intrusive memories, emotional numbness). Unsanctioned deep control, if discovered by the Conclave, carries severe consequences.

Example: You want to gently coax information from a frightened but untrained witness for a few minutes, nudging their fear away and trust toward you.

  • Depth: 1 (Emotional steering: calm them and build trust).
  • Clarity: 1 (Convincing: they think it is their own feeling).
  • Scope: 1 (Emotion + simple belief: “you can trust me.”)
  • Duration: 1 (Minutes: long enough for a conversation).
  • Resistance: 1 (Guarded / anxious but not trained).
  • Subtlety: 1 (Gentle guidance: they rationalize it).

Total difficulty = 6

You commit 5 mana and Cast Conclave Ritual. You roll 1d6[3] + 5 mana = 8 vs difficulty 6 and 1d10[9]. That is a weak hit: downgrade 1 factor (9 − 8 = 1). You drop Subtlety from 1 to 0 (crude push). The witness calms down and talks freely, but afterward feels uneasy: something about the conversation felt off, and they may later question why they were so open with a stranger.

Abilities:

  • You can cast compulsion spells to influence thoughts, emotions, and perceptions. Decide the effect you want, record the total difficulty by summing the difficulty factors, and Cast Conclave Ritual. You must spend at least 1 mana.
  • As above, but the spell difficulty is reduced by 2 (minimum 0) given mastery of the compulsion skill.
  • As above, but the spell difficulty is reduced by 4 instead of 2 (minimum 0).
