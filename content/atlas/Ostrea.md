# Ostrea

*City · Nysis · **Settled** · Oysters, whale oil, bone · pop. ~1,600 in the city, ~1,000 more along the strand*

**Type:** City · **Condition:** Well-kept · **Disposition:** Indifferent
**First look:** Foul stench · **Projects:** Hunting or fishing · **Touchstones:** Spirituality
**Region:** Ostrea · **Location:** Bay/Fjord
**Location detail:** A drowned valley on the north coast, at the head of the Nether Strand

**Exports:** Oysters by the barrel, live in seawater or damp weed for the near trade, salted and pickled in casks for anywhere further. Whale oil, which lights half of Nysis. Baleen and bone, sold by the ton and worked into everything from corset stays to roof-ribs. Burnt shell lime from the middens, for mortar and for sweetening sour fields inland.

**Imports:** Everything else. Grain and flour, because there is no arable ground here worth the name. Timber and barrel staves in enormous quantity, since the whole city runs on cooperage. Iron, cordage, canvas, cloth, wine, and every manufactured good, none of which Ostrea makes.

Ostrea sits at the top of the world, on the last shelter the Sea of Bees offers before the coast turns north into untamed wilderness. The harbour itself is a drowned valley: deep, cold, and narrow enough that a ship coming in loses the wind halfway and has to be warped the rest of the way to the quay by men on capstans. There is no beach in it and no shallow water at all. But east of the harbour mouth the land lies down, and for eleven miles the coast is flat, grey, and tidal, and that is where the city's food and most of its money come from.

The beds are worked two ways. At low water the flats belong to everybody: villagers out from the shore settlements with baskets and short rakes, gathering wild oysters by hand off the exposed ground, working fast in the four hours the tide allows and coming back up the strand in a long straggling line as the water turns. Deeper out, past the low-water mark, the ground is leased, and there the work is done from small open boats: a toothed iron rake on a rope, dragged along the bottom under sail, hauled up, sorted on the thwarts, the undersized ones flung back. Neither job is skilled. Both are hard, cold, and wet, and Ostrea has always had more people willing to do them than beds to do them on.

The leases are the city's real property. The urbestro or mayor grants them, against a rent paid in barrels at every quarter; the great beds off the Nether Strand (the Long Ground, Cauldwater, the Widow's Rake) have been held by the same families for six and seven generations and are inherited, mortgaged, and married over like farmland. A lease cannot be sold to an outsider. It can be forfeited, and the ground on which it is forfeited is that the bed has been let go barren, which is a judgement the urbestro makes and no one can appeal.

The oyster is not a delicacy here and never has been. It is bulk protein (cheap, abundant, eaten daily by people who eat little else) and its trade runs on the Ecclesia's calendar. A third of the year is abstinence, nothing warm-blooded may be eaten, and the bloodless rank above fish as the strict and creditable observance. That is Ostrea's market, and it is enormous, reliable, and set by a church rather than by appetite. Live stock is held in **parks**: shallow walled pits along the strand, flooded at every tide, where a day's dredging can sit for weeks waiting on a price or a fast day. What travels inland goes live: barrelled in seawater or packed in damp weed, out on the coast road under a wet sailcloth, and it will keep as long as it stays cool and closed.

Ostrea has the best harbour on this coast and the worst position to have it in. The drowned valley will take hulls that Piscis, sixty miles down the coast, must lighter ashore a boatload at a time, but Piscis sits on the road network and Ostrea sits at the end of it. So the two cities have spent two centuries covering each other. Deep-draft cargo consigned to Piscis is often landed at Ostrea's quay instead and carted down the coast road, two days by wagon, and everything Ostrea eats and builds with comes back up that same road. It is the only overland link either of them has that does not touch the blight.

The whale is the newer money and it belongs to fewer people. The northern grounds are four to eight weeks out and the try-works on the western shore render what comes back, and the four houses that own the ships (Ryke, Halbrand, Ostmarch and Vell) own most of the good stone in the upper city and, between them, the urbestro. Four houses is not many, and a ship or two apiece is not a fleet, but a whaler carries thirty men and Ostrea has perhaps four hundred grown ones. When the ships are out, a quarter of the city is out with them. Ostrea burns whale oil in its street lamps because it is cheaper here than tallow, and the smoke of the try-works settles into the valley on still days and gets into the bread. Between the many who gather and the few who render there is a fault line that Ostrea's extraordinary piety papers over and does not close. **Urbestro Maden Kelso** is chosen by the propertied (the leaseholders and the four houses) and the gatherers on the low water, who are the majority of the people here, have no say in it at all. He does not greatly need them to. Like every urbestro in Nysis he draws a share of the Conclave's annual payment for the Ravenna lease, and that income arrives whether Ostrea prospers or not, from a blight a hundred and ten miles away that he has never seen and never intends to.

The Ecclesia is strong here (a whaling town has a use for a faith that promises the sea can be endured) and the **Anvilhouse** on the upper terrace was raised from the take of a single remarkable season and has never let anyone forget it. It is not merely devout, it is commercial: the church sets the abstinence calendar, the calendar sets the demand, and the priests who publish the dates are the largest single buyer of barrelled oyster in Nysis. When the Anvilhouse and the four houses agree on something, the matter is closed before the urbestro hears of it. Touched are not welcome on the beds or in the boats, which in practice means not welcome. And the whole of it lies a hundred and ten miles from the nearest unblighted road south. Ostrea faces the water because there is nothing else to face.

**Features**
- A drowned-valley harbour, ships warped in by capstan
- Eleven miles of grey tidal flat east of the harbour mouth
- Gatherers out on the low water with baskets and short rakes
- Dredge boats under sail, rakes dragging, sorting on the thwarts
- Holding parks along the strand, flooding and draining twice a day
- Barrels of seawater and damp weed going out on the coast road
- Middens of shell, some of them older than the city
- Try-works smoking on the western shore, and whale-oil lamps
- Baleen and jawbone used as fenceposts, gateposts, roof-ribs
- Ecclesia bells on the hour, all night, in fog
- The abstinence calendar posted where everyone can read the dates
- Quarter-day rents carried up to the urbestro's hall in barrels
- The Anvilhouse above it all, and the four houses' stone around it
- The coast road south to Piscis, and wagons on it in all weather
- Weather arriving with two days' warning and no mercy

**Quest Starter**
> An oyster bed that has fed one family for six generations has gone dead: every shell in it empty, and the water above it clear in a way the older gatherers do not like. The next penitential week is eleven days off and the city has contracts it cannot fill. The family is certain it was poisoned by a rival lease. The Ecclesia is certain it is a judgement. Which is it, and what happens to whoever is blamed?
