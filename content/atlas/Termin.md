# Termin

*Town · Mososi · **Settled** · Coal, and the only overland crossing that matters · pop. ~1,600, plus whoever the tariff is currently holding*

**Type:** Town · **Condition:** Modest · **Disposition:** Demanding
**First look:** Visited by large caravan or fleet · **Projects:** Mining · **Touchstones:** Adherence to authority
**Region:** Termin · **Location:** Pass
**Location detail:** The gap between the eastern shore of Lake Mater and the foot of the Pinna

**Exports:** Coal, by the wagonload, east to the Pinna smelters that cannot get enough of it, and east a shorter way to the glass sheds at [[Vitre]] along with the fireclay that comes up from under the same seams. Beyond that Termin sells waiting: stabling, feed, warehousing, beds, and beer, to people who did not plan to be here this long.

**Imports:** Pinna iron and steel moving west into Mososi. Timber down the lake from [[Fluenti]]. Grain, because the coal country grows nothing worth eating. Very little else, and that is the point.

Termin means *the limit*, and it is one: the last Mososi town before the Pinna Mountains, wedged in the gap between the eastern shore of Lake Mater and the rising ground, with the Nysis border running away south-east as a line of cairns that everybody argues about. It is the only place two countries' worth of overland traffic can cross without going a very long way around, and both countries have spent two centuries making that crossing as expensive as they can bear.

So the tariff is the town. Duty is assessed both ways on nearly everything, on schedules revised often enough that no one can memorise them; goods are weighed, bonded, sealed, inspected, held, and released when a clerk gets to them. A wagon can sit three days for want of a stamp. Nothing about this is corruption. It is policy, applied by competent people who are not permitted to hurry, and it is mirrored a day's walk east by a Nysisian post doing exactly the same thing in the opposite direction.

The consequence is that anything valuable enough to be worth freight goes around by water instead, out through Freeport and back in on the far side, and arrives cheaper and sooner than it would have crossed here. What comes through Termin is what the sea cannot carry economically: coal, ore, pig iron, timber, grain in a bad year, cattle on the hoof. The traffic is enormous and almost all of it is bulk. That makes for a town loud with wagons and thin on merchants: teamsters, drovers, hostlers, weighers, and clerks, and not a factor or a broker worth the name in the place. The smart money is on a barge somewhere south, and everyone in Termin knows it.

The coal underneath gives it a floor regardless. Shallow seams run under the low ground east of town, worked by drift and adit rather than shaft, men walking in on the level, following a seam until the roof gets bad, then starting another. It is dirtier work than the Pinna iron and less respected, and it never stops. Coal dust is in the mortar, in the bread, and in the lungs of anyone who has been here twenty years.

Mososi collects here through a *prefekto* appointed out of Typpe, salaried, rotated, and forbidden to be flexible. Nysis collects through an urbestro who is nobody's employee and answers to no one, and who draws a share of the Conclave's rent for Ravenna every year on top of it, for a blight he has never visited. The two officials are correct with each other and nothing more. The strip of ground between their posts is where a certain amount of commerce happens that neither country has a form for, and where, for eleven years, six furnaces have been burning. [[Vitre]] is a day east and it is not in anyone's ledger: seven hundred people making the best glass in Yrt on ground that both capitals claim and neither will govern, because governing it means conceding that it could have been governed by the other. Termin sells it coal and clay and buys nothing back, and the two officials have between them never once written the name down.

**Features**
- Wagon yards full of vehicles that are not going anywhere today
- Coal dust in the mortar, the bread, and the lungs
- Drift mouths in the low ground, timbered and dripping
- The weigh house scale, and the argument permanently around it
- Bonded warehouses carrying two sets of seals
- Duty schedules posted, amended, and posted again
- Teamsters and drovers; no merchant of any consequence
- Border cairns running south-east, some of them moved
- Braziers of slack coal burning in the street all winter
- Lake Mater flat and grey to the west, with barges on it going the other way

**Quest Starter**
> A cairn on the border line has been moved about eighty yards, and the ground it moved is worth something to somebody. Both officials know. Neither will report it, because whoever reports it first admits their own side was not watching. Then a Mososi carter is killed on the disputed strip, and it can no longer be left alone.
