# Suda Forest

*Boundary · Nysis / Mososi · Pine, pitch, tar, turpentine; the crossing nobody admits to*

**Region:** Suda Forest · **Location:** Woods
**Location detail:** South of the Pinna, on the sandy ridges where the hardwood gives out

South of the Pinna range, where the mountains break down into long sandy ridges, the hardwood gives out and the pine takes over. The Suda is a drier, brighter, poorer forest than the [[Nebula Forest|Nebula]]: open stands of tall red-barked pine on thin soil, floored in needle litter, hot in summer and loud with insects, with the ground showing white between the trees. You can see a long way in the Suda. This is the first thing everyone notices and the reason it feels safer than it is.

It is also the quietest ground in the region. A foot of needle litter takes every sound a boot makes and gives nothing back, and the insects cover what is left. The Nebula takes your eyes and leaves you your ears. The Suda does the opposite, and people who know both will tell you the Suda is worse, and cannot always say why.

## Naval Stores

The trade is resin, and everything downstream of it.

A mature pine is worked by cutting a **box** low in the trunk (a hollow that will hold a season's flow) and then chipping a fresh streak above it every week or so, because a cut that is allowed to dry stops running. The gum comes down the face and collects, and a crew walking its ground does nothing all season but chip, empty, and carry. A good tree runs for years before it is worked out. A tree worked too hard dies standing and is left where it is.

The crude gum goes to the stills (squat clay and copper affairs set up wherever the season's ground happens to be) and comes off as **spirits of turpentine**, with **rosin** left behind in the pot. Separately, and messier, resinous heartwood and stumpwood are stacked in a covered **earth kiln** and burned slow and starved of air for days, so that **tar** sweats out and runs down a clay channel into a barrel at the low end. Boil that tar down further and it becomes **pitch**.

All of it goes into ships. Pitch and tar for the seams and the standing rigging at [[Freeport]] and for every hull [[Ostrea]] sends after whales; turpentine for lamps, for paint, for medicine of varying honesty; rosin for a dozen trades. The Refiners' Guild takes a quantity every year for purposes it does not put in writing. The barrels stack on the south road and go out by cart, and the crews are paid by the barrel and not by the day.

## Fire

Every few years the Suda burns, sometimes for a month, and the crews watch the smoke and calculate.

They are not simply being callous. A pine forest on sand *wants* fire: a ground fire runs through the litter, clears the scrub, opens the cones, and leaves the mature trees standing with their thick bark scorched and their crowns untouched. The Suda has burned like this for longer than there have been people in it and comes back better each time. Every resin worker knows this, and a burn that takes the understory and spares the stand is not a disaster but a season's inconvenience.

What they are less willing to say out loud is that the industry manufactures the other kind. A tapped face is bare wood soaked in turpentine, and it does not smoulder. It catches, and it burns hot enough and high enough to take the crown, and a tree that loses its crown is dead. A stand under tap is a stand that has been made into kindling one trunk at a time. Everyone in the Suda understands this perfectly and taps anyway, because a tree left alone this season is a tree the next crew boxes, and nobody has ever held ground here by being careful with it.

The old burn scars carry the record: standing black snags in rows, still showing the box cut that caught.

## The Border That Isn't

Nobody governs the Suda. The Nysis–Mososi border is understood to run through it somewhere and has never been agreed, because agreeing it would mean one country conceding the tapping rights and neither will. So the Suda is administered by whoever is standing in it: crews hold their ground by presence and by arrangement, disputes are settled among the crews, and both governments have found this so convenient that neither has any intention of fixing it.

The convenience is not about resin. It is that a border nobody has drawn is a border nobody can man.

[[Termin]] is the only overland crossing that matters, and its customs regime is punishing by design: everything weighed, bonded, inspected, held, and released on a clerk's timetable, with the schedules revised often enough that no broker can memorise them. Bulk goods do not attempt it; they go round by water through Freeport, which is cheaper even with the transhipment. But there is a third category, and it walks through the Suda.

It has to be small, because there are no roads here and no bridges and a wagon gets four miles before the sand defeats it. It has to be dear, because a man carrying it is walking eleven days. That narrows the traffic to a very specific list (seed, spirits, fine cloth, coin, letters, and people) and every item on that list is precisely what the tariff at Termin is most punitive about. The route is not a secret. It is simply not a road, and therefore not a thing either country has to have a position on.

## The Crews

They come in for the season, build nothing, and leave. Chippers, dippers, kiln men, coopers, a still crew, and whoever is walking the barrels out. They are the poorest paid skilled workers in the region and they are paid better than they would be anywhere they came from.

The Suda does not ask questions, and this is its one real mercy. There is no magistrate to register with, no inquisitor at any gate, no Ecclesia house within four days' walk and no reason for one to come. A Touched man who cannot get hired in [[Collima]] can chip resin in the Suda and be judged entirely on whether his trees run, and a great many of them do exactly that. It is hot, filthy, seasonal, dangerous work in a forest that periodically catches fire, and for a certain kind of person in this region it is the best offer available.

**Features**
- Open stands of tall red-barked pine, white ground between
- Box cuts low on every mature trunk, chipped fresh each week
- Clay and copper stills wherever this season's ground happens to be
- Earth kilns burning slow, and tar running down a channel into a barrel
- Needle litter that takes every sound and gives nothing back
- Long sightlines that make you careless
- Old burn scars with black snags standing in rows
- A tapped face, bare and soaked, and what it does in a fire
- Seasonal camps, no permanent building anywhere
- Barrel stacks waiting on the south road
- A border that exists on paper and nowhere on the ground
- Men walking north with packs too small to be worth eleven days

**Quest Starter**
> Two crews are working the same stand, both with a legitimate arrangement: one made in Nysis, one made in Mososi. The Suda has always settled this among itself. This time one of the crews has hired men who are not resin workers, and the season has four months left to run.
