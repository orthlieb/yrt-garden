# Beyond the Sea of Bees

*Remote · Biel, Ramage, Gelandia · Three places the region trades with and has never seen · off-oracle*

**Region:** Sea of Bees · **Location:** Sea
**Location detail:** Somewhere out past the northern grounds, on courses no local house owns a chart for

Three countries send hulls across the [[Sea of Bees]] and take hulls back. The region knows their names, knows what comes off them, knows what they will pay for, and knows almost nothing else, and this entry is a fair account of that ignorance rather than an attempt to fix it.

Everything below is drawn from three sources and no others. The Grand Office's book of ship's listings at [[Freeport]], which records a flag code, a rig, a home port and a reputation. The Mint, which will change foreign coin and therefore has an opinion about who is good for it. And captains, in taverns, who have every reason to be interesting.

Nobody in this region has been to any of them and come back with a chart. What arrives is cargo and the story that came with it.

## Biel

**The rocky island.** Comes in three or four times a year in deep-draft hulls, and never in winter.

What it sends is **stone**, dressed and graded: millstones above all, and whetstones, and a hard pale grit sold by the barrel for polishing. Every mill in the region turns on Biel stone, which is not a figure of speech. The grain mills at [[Typpe]], the saws at [[Fluenti]], the ore mill at [[Ravenna]], the plate-grinders at [[Mons]]: all of them running on rock cut off an island none of them could point to. The stone takes a face that local rock will not hold, wears flat rather than glazing, and can be dressed a dozen times before it is done. Nobody here has found a substitute and the Refiners' Guild has looked.

What it takes back is everything an island of rock does not grow. Grain, timber, salt fish, cloth, oil, iron in bar. The trade balance is entirely in Biel's favour and has been for as long as the Mint has kept rates.

What a captain will tell you: that the whole place is one quarry worked in tiers, that there are no trees on it at all, and that the harbour is a cut rather than a bay. He will not tell you how long the passage takes, and the listings show Biel hulls clearing Freeport with provisions for considerably longer than they admit to needing.

## Ramage

**The strange archipelago.** Comes irregularly, in shallow-draft hulls that have no business crossing open water and cross it anyway.

What it sends is **dye**: a deep blue-black that does not fade, sold in sealed jars by weight and worth more than its weight in most things, along with a red that is cheaper and a lacquer-gum that hardens in air. Freeport's dyers buy the lot and will not discuss what they pay. It also sends cordage fibre, long and rot-resistant, which the [[Ostrea]] whalers prefer over anything local and pay over the odds for.

What it takes back is grain, worked metal, glass, and every kind of tool.

What is strange about Ramage is not the goods. It is that every hull is built to the same pattern, down to the rig, so that a Ramage boat is recognisable from the horizon and always has been. That no manifest from a Ramage hull has ever been found wrong at a rummage, in the whole of the Grand Office's book. And that the crews take their pay in Freeport, spend it in Freeport, and do not sleep ashore.

Freeport finds this restful and has never enquired further. A customs desk that has never once caught you in an error will stop looking for one, which is a thing worth knowing about Freeport as well as about Ramage.

## Gelandia

**The icy reaches.** Comes twice a year, and the second sailing is a gamble that some years does not arrive.

What it sends is **fur** in quality nothing here can match, **sea-ivory** by the tusk, down, and oil of a kind Ostrea's try-works does not produce. What it takes back is grain, above everything, and after that iron, cloth and worked timber.

The thing nobody at [[Ostrea]] says out loud is that Gelandia is where the northern grounds are. Ostrea's whalers go four to eight weeks north and work water that the Gelandian hulls also work, and both sides have been doing it long enough to have arrangements about it, and Ostrea's four houses describe those arrangements to nobody. A whaling master will tell you the grounds are open water and no one's. He will also tell you which reaches to keep off in which season, and he did not work that out by himself.

## How Much Of This To Believe

The listings are reliable about flags, rigs and debts, because a house that got those wrong would stop being paid. Everything else in this entry is a captain's word.

Distances are the worst of it. Nobody sells a chart of the northern water at Freeport, the Conclave has never funded a survey, and the one man in the Grand Office who has read every listing back forty years will tell you, if you buy him enough, that the sailing times do not reconcile with each other and never have.

**Features**
- Deep-draft hulls at Dock Walk under flags nobody local can draw from memory
- Biel millstones stacked on the quay, graded and chalked
- Sealed dye jars weighed at the customs desk under a clerk's full attention
- Ramage hulls, identical to one another, standing off and not landing a crew
- Fur bales and tusks coming ashore in the same week every autumn
- Foreign coin at the Mint, and a rate that moved since last season
- Provision lists that do not match the passages anybody admits to
- Sailing times in the Grand Office book that have never reconciled

**Quest Starter**
> A Biel hull has come in a month early, out of season, riding light, and its master wants to buy grain at any price and leave inside two days. He will not say what happened and the listings show his house has never missed a season in ninety years. Freeport's factors smell a shortage and are already moving on the grain price. Whatever is wrong is four weeks out and nobody in this city owns a chart that reaches it.
