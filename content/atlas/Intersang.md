# Intersang

*Village · Buralia · **Boundary** · The march's wharf; no customs house and one law · pop. ~300, and three times that at a landing*

**Type:** Village · **Condition:** Modest · **Disposition:** Wary
**First look:** Boundary of standing stones · **Projects:** Trade · **Touchstones:** Strict laws
**Region:** Falter Bay · **Location:** Coast
**Location detail:** The south-eastern shore of Falter Bay, twenty-five miles down the isthmus from Freeport and forty miles by road below Mons

**Exports:** What [[Mons]] has bulked and sent down: obsidian in blades, ground plate and mirrors, sulphur, pumice, and the crystal out of the high vents. Under that, everything the fen has been persuaded to part with, in bales: dried fungus, medicinals, hides, horn, feathers.

**Imports:** Grain, and then more grain. After that salt, iron, cloth and cordage, all of it Mososi, all of it across the water, none of it ever having seen [[Freeport]].

Intersang is the [[Buralia|march]]'s answer to a question nobody in the march asked out loud: what is the shortest distance between an obsidian quarry and somebody with food.

It turns out to be forty miles of road down from [[Mons]] and forty miles of water across the Bay to [[Lokigi]], and neither leg touches Freeport's canal, and both together take less than a week. The ash country has been selling east to the tribute train for forty-seven years because it was told to. It has been selling west across this water for rather longer than that, because it wanted to.

The wharf is two hundred feet of stacked stone and squared timber, built by nobody in particular over three generations, repaired by whoever needs it to hold next season.

## The Ring

There is no customs house at Intersang. There is no ledger, no weigh house, no register of hulls, and no official of any kind. What there is, is a ring of set stones on the shore, and the [[Mons|Gards]] who walk it.

Inside the ring, violence is forbidden. The penalty is not a fine and not a rope: it is exclusion from Mons, which for a march trader is the end of trading, because Mons is the only market where the fen can get salt and where glass is worth carrying anywhere. That is the entire legal apparatus of this place and it has held since before the empire arrived. A man who has been robbed at Intersang has been robbed twenty yards outside the stones, and knew where the stones were.

The Gards keep it because they keep Mons, and the Gards are Wildens, Kroduk's. So the most reliable wharf on Falter Bay is policed by the faith [[Freeport]] drives underground, twenty-five miles from the customs office that would like a word, and [[Typpe]]'s barge masters land here twice a week and take the arrangement as they find it. An Optime country does not grade people and does not ask whose god keeps the peace it is standing in. A Nyssian factor would not set foot on the stones and does not have to, because Nysis has nothing to sell here.

## What Does Not Land

Slaving is lawful throughout Buralia and no rope comes onto this wharf.

That is commerce and nobody here pretends it is anything else. Mososi will not carry them, Mososi is the only customer on the far shore, and a trade good with no buyer is not a trade good. The takings go east overland with the tribute train, the way they always have, and the water stays clean because the water pays better clean.

The exception arrives every seventh spring. When the **Clearing** is called, every slave in the region goes free on the day, and a proportion of them walk. Some of them walk here, because a wharf is the only place on this frontier where a man who owns nothing can get onto a boat, and by the second week the stones are ringed with people who want to cross and have no way to pay for it. Mososi has never settled what its policy is. In 225 the barges took some and refused others and nobody has ever explained the sorting. The next Clearing is in 232, which is two years off, and both shores know it.

## The Name

Freeport's factors write it *Intersang* in the day book and will tell you, if pressed, that it is a worn-down trade word, an exchange, that came up the road with the first grain and lost its ending on the way.

The ash country says it is where the bloods meet, and means the peoples, not the killing.

Both are current, both are used at the same wharf on the same morning, and the Gards have never once been asked to rule on it.

**Features**
- Two hundred feet of stacked stone wharf, patched in three generations of work
- A ring of set stones on the shore, and what stops at the edge of it
- Gards walking the ring, unarmed, and nobody testing why
- Obsidian plate crated in straw, and mirrors carried by two men
- Sulphur in yellow heaps under oiled canvas, and the smell of it in the village
- Mososi barges twice a week, low with grain going one way and glass the other
- No weigh house, no clerk, no ledger anywhere in the place
- Bales of fen goods that came forty miles down a road and forty across water
- Freeport's smoke visible up the shore on a still day, twenty-five miles off
- Every seventh spring, people on the beach with nothing, waiting on a barge master's judgement

**Quest Starter**
> A man has been killed inside the ring, which has not happened at Intersang in the memory of anyone standing on it. The Gards must name someone before the next landing or the Peace is finished here and everybody knows it. They have a name. It belongs to a factor whose grain contract feeds the whole of the lower ash country through winter, and Mons will not survive the naming any better than the wharf will survive the silence. The next Mososi barge is due in four days.
