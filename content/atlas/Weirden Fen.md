# Weirden Fen

*Remote · Buralia · Fruit, medicinals, fungus, eels, alder*

**Exports** — Dried fen fruit by the barrel. Medicinals, sold to the Azure Mendicants at prices that fund whole platforms. Fungus, in a dozen grades from ordinary eating to preparations the Conclave has views about. Smoked eel. And alder piling, which does not rot underwater and which [[Freeport]] buys by the shipload because Freeport is built on a sinking isthmus.

**Imports** — Grain, salt, iron, cloth. Everything that will not grow in standing water.

The Weirden is a fen and not a bog, and the fen-folk will correct you on it. A bog is fed by rain, sour and starved, and grows sphagnum and heather and very little a person can eat. The Weirden is fed from below, out of the limestone under the ash country, and the water that comes up through it is hard, cold and rich. That is the whole reason sixty miles of standing water is worth anything at all. What would be a wasteland is instead the most productive ground in western Buralia, and none of it is dry.

It goes in three depths. At the margins, open water and **reed** — beds of it running for a mile at a stretch, with reedmace, bur-reed, yellow flag and water-lily at the edges, and bladderwort underneath quietly eating whatever swims into it. Inland of that the **sedge fen**: great fen-sedge tall enough to hide a boat and edged like a saw, tussock sedge standing in hummocks solid enough to take a man's weight, and between them bogbean, marsh cinquefoil, meadowsweet, valerian, water mint, purple loosestrife, and orchids that people come a long way to steal. Deeper in it closes over into **carr** — alder standing in the water on stilted roots, grey willow and osier, downy birch on the hummocks, guelder rose, alder buckthorn, and bog myrtle, which is burned in every platform hearth in the fen because the smoke is the only thing the insects respect. Where the ground lifts clear there are **holms**, dry islands carrying oak and ash and hazel, and every one of them is somebody's.

What comes out of it is worth the trouble. Fruit — cranberry, bilberry, elder, blackcurrant, dewberry — dried on racks and barrelled. Fungus off the drowned alder in water that never moves: oyster and velvet shank for the pot, birch polypore for a wound, and the fire-fungi, amadou and cramp-ball, which smoulder for half a day and are how anybody carries fire across sixty miles of wet. Eels, taken in willow traps and smoked, which is what the fen actually eats. And the medicinals, which cut both ways: meadowsweet and willow bark for pain and fever, marsh mallow root, comfrey for a broken bone — and, on the same rounds and often within a few yards, cowbane and hemlock water-dropwort, which will kill a grown man from a mouthful and are collected by the same families, for different customers. The Mendicants and the Brotherhood's poisoners are supplied out of the same baskets.

The fen-folk live on platforms and in shallow-draft boats and are the only people who move through the interior with any confidence. **Collecting rounds** are inherited like property — a named route through named water, with the good stands and the safe channels and the killing ground all held in one family's head and passed down by walking it. They pay their tribute in kind, carry their goods to [[Mons]] where the Peace lets them trade with people they would otherwise avoid, and conduct their own affairs with a self-sufficiency that visiting officials call insolence and experienced ones call the arrangement. Every few years somebody drowns a stranger for going where they were told not to go, and nobody comes about it.

**Features**
- Standing black water under alder, out of sight in every direction
- Reedbeds a mile deep, and the noise of them in wind
- Fen-sedge that cuts, and tussocks that carry a man's weight
- Bog myrtle smoke in every hearth, and the insects just outside it
- Alder on stilted roots, standing in the water
- Holms of dry oak and hazel, every one of them owned
- Drying racks of fruit on every platform
- Fungus on drowned wood where the water never moves
- Willow eel-traps, and smoke-houses going all season
- Flat-bottomed boats poled down channels a shoulder wide
- Collecting rounds inherited, walked, and never written down
- A channel that was not there last year, and one that no longer is
- Coasters going home from Freeport heavier than a return leg should be

**Quest Starter**
> A family's collecting round has been walked by somebody else — carefully, in the right order, taking only what a rightful holder would take. That knowledge is not written anywhere and cannot be learned from outside. Either somebody sold it, or somebody who died with it did not die.
