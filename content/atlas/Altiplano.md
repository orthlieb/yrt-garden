# Altiplano

*City · Buralia · **Settled** · Imperial seat; herds, terraced grain, records · pop. ~6,000, and four times that when the court is in · off-oracle*

**Type:** Capital · **Condition:** Grand · **Disposition:** Indifferent
**First look:** Built into the terrain · **Projects:** History · **Touchstones:** Adherence to authority
**Region:** Altiplano · **Location:** Cliff
**Location detail:** Mesas above a sea of grass, south-east beyond the edge of this Atlas

**Exports:** Very little that reaches this region, and nothing that reaches it recognisably. Wool and worked cloth, hardy grain, dried tuber flour, silver plate, and law: the empire's writs, licences and judgments, which travel further than any of its goods.

**Imports:** Everything the high country will not grow, hauled a very long way: salt, timber, iron, sugar, oil, and such Freeport curiosities as survive the journey and arrive as gifts rather than cargo.

Altiplano is where Buralia keeps its records and its winter, and no one in the Freeport region has been there. It stands weeks to the south-east, beyond everything this Atlas covers, on the western edge of the grass: a dry high plain running out of sight in every direction, cold at night, fierce by day, with mesas of red sandstone standing out of it like the last pieces of something. The air is thin and startlingly clear, and the sky at that height is a colour that people from the coast describe badly and at length.

## What Is Actually Here

A city cut into cliffs, and a hole in the year.

For most of the year Altiplano is quiet: perhaps six thousand people, clerks and herders and the households that serve them, going about a place that is plainly built for more. Then the court comes back for the calving and the basin fills. Twenty-odd thousand arrive with the Emperor and stay while the herds drop their young, and the terraces below the mesas turn into a camp that can be seen from a day's ride, and every price in the city doubles. Then the season turns, the itinerary starts again, and the whole thing goes east and leaves a city rattling in its own walls.

Nobody here finds this strange. Altiplano is not the capital in the sense a Freeport man means. It is where the ledgers live and where the Emperor sleeps in winter, and the empire itself is somewhere else, moving.

## The City

It is built into the faces of three mesas and across the saddles between them, tier on tier, in the stone it is cut from, so that from below it reads as a change in the texture of the cliff rather than as a city. The oldest quarters are highest. Getting up is by ladder and cut stair, and everything that arrives arrives on somebody's back or a pack animal's, because there is no grade in Altiplano a cart can take. The wealthy live high, the poor live low, and the entire social order is legible as an elevation.

Water comes off the mesa tops by cut channel and is allocated by an office founded in the second reign, which has now outlasted four Emperors and two attempts to abolish it. Below the city the basin is terraced for hardy grain and tuber, and past the terraces the grass carries the herds (wool, meat, hide and haulage) which are counted every year by the same office that counts everything else.

## The Archive

The thing that would strike a visitor from this region hardest is not the altitude or the stone. It is the paperwork, and the reason for the paperwork is that the government is never here.

A court that arrives in a region it last stood in nine years ago cannot rule out of anybody's head. It has to know what was taken last time, what was promised, which governor was left, what he has sent since, and who has died. So the clerks count, the count comes back to the mesas, and it goes into vaults cut in dry red rock where paper keeps for centuries without anybody doing anything clever. The civil service sits examinations on the contents. A dispute in Buralia is settled by looking it up.

It is the best archive in the known world and it is a hundred and forty years deep. There is nothing in it from before the first Emperor, and an Altiplano clerk will tell you so before you ask, because the depth is not the point. The point is that it is complete.

Which is why the empire's frontier policy looks so strange from [[Mons]] and looks entirely reasonable from here. The march is a line in a ledger: a rim that costs nothing to hold, produces obsidian and sulphur and a tribute train that arrives every year, and is administered by not administering it. A governor is sent there when he has disappointed somebody. The [[Spero]] rent arrives in that same ledger, punctual, in silver, filed under *mission concession, worthless district*, and the man whose job it is to file it has never been within four hundred miles of the place and has no reason to think anybody has lied to him.

## The Cages

The court travels with a menagerie and it winters here, in pens cut along the second terrace where the rock holds the day's heat.

What is in them are elder beasts, taken alive out of wherever the itinerary reached, at a cost in men that the ledger records and does not comment on. They are fed, they are watched, and a keeper's post is inherited. In the calving season the Emperor walks the line most evenings, alone, and talks to them.

Altiplano is entirely used to this and a visitor is not. Nobody in the city treats it as a curiosity, and nobody explains it either, and the one question an outsider wants to ask is the one nobody here has ever thought to ask, which is whether anything answers.

## What It Does Not Know

Altiplano's strength is its memory and its weakness is that memory is all it has. It knows exactly what it was told and it filed that carefully.

It does not know that the mission concession is a refinery. It does not know that the labour walking through it is being imported by sea from a foreign country and worked to death on imperial ground. It does not know that the [[Mons|Gards]] have become the only functioning institution on its own frontier, or that the Clearing they enforce is a legal system the empire never authorised and could not now dislodge. And it does not know that a well-run province with pretensions is quietly feeding itself out of a Verdant seep the Conclave has been asking about for a hundred years.

None of this is in the ledgers, which is the one place an imperial official would think to look.

**Features**
- Mesas of red sandstone standing out of a sea of grass
- A city cut into the cliff faces, reading as texture until you are close
- Ladders and cut stair, and not a cart anywhere in it
- The wealthy high, the poor low, and the order visible at a glance
- Thin bright air, fierce sun, cold nights, and a sky that is the wrong colour
- Water channels off the mesa tops, allocated by an office older than the reign
- Terraced grain and tuber below, herds on the grass beyond
- Archive vaults cut in dry rock, holding every year since the founding
- Clerks who have sat examinations on the contents
- Beast pens along the second terrace, and keepers who inherit the post
- A city built for four times the people in it, ten months a year
- Frontier tribute logged annually and thought about never

**Quest Starter**
> Somebody in Altiplano has actually read the Spero file (the rent, the concession, the twelve years, the punctuality) and worked out that a mission does not pay that much for ground nobody wants. He cannot raise it, because raising it means explaining why the empire has been taking the money, and he has no proof and no authority and four hundred miles of frontier between him and the answer. What he has is a modest budget and permission to send somebody who is not an imperial official.

**Second Quest Starter**
> A keeper on the second terrace has lost an animal. Not escaped: the pen is shut and the lock is sound and the cage is empty, and it was one of the ones taken off a blight fringe. The court arrives for the calving in nine days. He has not reported it, because a keeper's post is inherited and his son is eleven.
