# Altiplano

*City · Buralia · **Settled** · Imperial capital; terraced grain, herds, records · pop. ~6,000 · off-oracle*

**Exports:** Very little that reaches this region, and nothing that reaches it recognisably. Wool and worked cloth, hardy grain, dried tuber flour, silver plate, and law: the empire's writs, licences and judgments, which travel further than any of its goods.

**Imports:** Everything the high country will not grow, hauled a very long way: salt, timber, iron, sugar, oil, and such Freeport curiosities as survive the journey and arrive as gifts rather than cargo.

Altiplano is the capital of Buralia and no one in the Freeport region has been there. It stands weeks to the south-east, beyond everything this Atlas covers, on a high plain among mesas: flat-topped tables of red rock standing out of a cold grassy basin at an altitude where a lowlander arrives with a headache and keeps it for a week. The air is thin and startlingly clear, the sun is fierce and the shade is cold, and the sky at that height is a colour that people from the coast describe badly and at length.

## Why The Empire Is Here

Because the Unmaking mostly happened somewhere else.

The Unmade built where the water, the ports and the people were, which is to say low and warm. What went wrong went wrong down there. These basins were thin-aired, dry, remote and of no industrial interest whatever, and they came through the Fall with their terraces, their herds, their water and (the part that matters) a working chain of authority. Not intact. Nothing was intact. But continuous.

That is the entire basis of Buralia's standing. Altiplano can name its rulers back past the Unmaking without inventing any of them, and it can produce the documents. Nowhere else in the known world can do either. An imperial official who has read the registers finds [[Freeport]] a two-hundred-year-old trading camp, [[Typpe|Mososi]] a well-run province with pretensions, and [[Nysis]] a committee of mayors, and is not being rude; he is describing what the records show.

## The City

It is built up the flanks and across the saddles between three mesas, in stone the colour of the rock it is cut from, so that from below it reads as a change in the texture of the cliff rather than as a city. The oldest quarters are on top. Getting up is by ramps and cut stair, and everything that arrives (every sack, every beam, every barrel) arrives on somebody's back or a pack animal's, because there is no grade in Altiplano a cart can take. The wealthy live high, the poor live low, and the entire social order is legible as an elevation.

Water comes off the mesas by cut channel and is allocated by an office that predates the current dynasty and has outlasted three that failed. Below the city the basin is terraced for hardy grain and tuber, and beyond the terraces the cold grassland carries the herds (wool, meat, hide and haulage) which are counted annually by the same office that counts everything else.

## The Archive

The thing that would strike a visitor from this region hardest is not the altitude or the stone. It is the paperwork.

Buralia keeps records, has kept them continuously, and stores them here in vaults cut into the mesa where the air is dry enough to preserve paper for centuries without anybody doing anything clever. Land, water, herds, taxes, judgments, appointments, the census, and the frontier's tribute: all of it written down, filed, cross-referenced, and consulted. The civil service sits examinations on the contents. A dispute in Buralia is settled by looking it up.

Which is why the empire's frontier policy looks so strange from Mons and looks entirely reasonable from here. The march is a *line in a ledger*: a rim that costs nearly nothing to hold, produces obsidian and sulphur and a modest tribute, and is administered by not administering it. A governor is sent there when he has disappointed somebody. The [[Spero]] rent arrives in that same ledger, punctual, in silver, filed under *mission concession, worthless district*, and the man whose job it is to file it has never been within four hundred miles of the place and has no reason to think anybody has lied to him.

## What It Does Not Know

Altiplano's strength is its memory and its weakness is that memory is all it has. It knows exactly what it was told and it filed that carefully.

It does not know that the mission concession is a refinery. It does not know that the labour walking through it is being imported by sea from a foreign country and worked to death on imperial ground. It does not know that the Gardistoy at [[Mons]] have become the only functioning institution on its own frontier, or that the Clearing they enforce is a legal system the empire never authorised and could not now dislodge. And it does not know that a well-run province with pretensions is quietly feeding itself out of a Verdant seep the Conclave has been asking about for a hundred years.

None of this is in the registers, which is the one place an imperial official would think to look.

**Features**
- Mesas of red rock standing out of a cold grassy basin
- A city cut from the same stone, reading as texture in the cliff
- Ramps and cut stair, and not a cart anywhere in it
- The wealthy high, the poor low, and the order visible at a glance
- Thin bright air, fierce sun, cold shade, and a sky that is the wrong colour
- Water channels off the mesa tops, allocated by an office older than the dynasty
- Terraced grain and tuber below, herds on the grassland beyond
- Archive vaults cut into dry rock, holding four centuries of paper
- Clerks who have sat examinations on the contents
- Frontier tribute logged annually and thought about never
- Envoys from countries the registers describe as recent
- A confidence that is not arrogance and reads exactly like it

**Quest Starter**
> Somebody in Altiplano has actually read the Spero file (the rent, the concession, the twelve years, the punctuality) and worked out that a mission does not pay that much for ground nobody wants. He cannot raise it, because raising it means explaining why the empire has been taking the money, and he has no proof and no authority and four hundred miles of frontier between him and the answer. What he has is a modest budget and permission to send somebody who is not an imperial official.
