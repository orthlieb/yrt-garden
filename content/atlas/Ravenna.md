# Ravenna

*Remote · Nysis · Blight; Conclave mana extraction*

**Region:** Ravenna · **Location:** Waste
**Location detail:** Inland between the shore and the northern Pinna, north of Collima

Ravenna is where the region's mana comes from and it is the price the region pays for having any. The blight covers the country either side of the lower Duforq, between the Falter Sound shore and the northern Pinna, and it is not a place so much as a gradient: the ground gets worse, the vegetation gets stranger, the animals get larger and more deliberate, and at some point along that gradient you are in it. There is no line and no gate. The road runs through and the road is the only ordinary thing on it.

The Conclave holds the extraction monopoly here by state grant and pays Nysis heavily for it every year, and the monopoly is administered from three numbered Mana Refinement Sites strung along the river. **Site \#3** is the deep site and the largest. **Site \#2** is active and fortified, and its watchtowers are the first built thing most travellers see. **Site \#1** sat at the Duforq bridge and processed westward-flowing mana before river transport, and it has been abandoned since 140 SU, the Year of the Black Rain, when a trough went hard-steam and worked down into a single-lined black well and the well let go. Fourteen died where they stood. Corrosive material fell for hundreds of yards. Ninety years of Verdant reclamation have grown over the foundations in bloodthorn and the residual Black is still there and nobody has proposed reopening it. Guild hands do not discuss Site One.

Between the sites is the working blight, and it is worked by people. Rakers, trough hands, brass-crown operators, sieve crews, the drying fields. Nothing of value leaves this place out of the ground: raw manite is inert, and it is the trough line that makes it worth carrying, which is the whole basis of the monopoly and the reason the fence runs where it does. And nothing much leaves off the line either. Two locks on the vault held by two people, weights taken at four stages and reconciled by clerks who answer to Freeport rather than the site, a body search at the gate on every shift, and the Guild counting alongside because a foreman caught short-weighing is a foreman the Guild can remove. Men do get out with a thimble of amber in a boot heel once in a while and are caught more often than not. It is a trickle and it has always been a trickle. Everyone who stays gets **drift** eventually: the slow change in how a person thinks and feels and mends that long exposure produces and that arrives too gradually to notice from the inside. And the Verdani have been raiding the sites with increasing frequency and increasing seriousness, because the extraction is eating ground they consider theirs, and they have concluded that talking about it did not work.

## The Guild

Ravenna is not survivable because the Conclave is careful. It is survivable because in 142 SU, after the sites killed enough people to make the deaths politically expensive, the order was made to accept the **Freeport Refiner's Guild**, and the compact it signed, the **Concordat of Labor and Light**, is the single most important document in the blight. The Conclave conceded the Guild legal standing. The Guild conceded the Conclave operational authority inside the fence. Everything either party has done since has been an argument about where that line falls.

What the Guild actually does is keep a second set of books. It inspects every active site quarterly, it records conditions, casualties and incidents in its own hand, and it does not show those records to the order. It commissions its own training documents. It appoints an under-foreman at each site as a representative, whose job is to collect complaints and route them upward through the proper channel: dull, unglamorous, and the reason the process has teeth.

It works. It worked most recently on a senior foreman at Site \#3 whose accident rate ran above Site \#2's and whose crews put in more overtime than anyone could account for, and who was quietly running an industrial-scale compulsion on his own workers. No individual complaint proved anything. Eighteen months of them, funnelled up by a Guild rep who never editorialised and never accused, crossed the Guild's threshold for formal escalation, and a grievance was filed, and an inspector was assigned, and the man was recalled.

That is the system working exactly as designed. What happened next is not the Guild's fault and is the reason this section belongs in the entry at all: the Conclave, presented with a monster its own institution had caught, chose to handle it privately. No charges, no public finding, no notice to anyone outside the order. A dismissal, a stripped crown beaten into slag in the yard at Site \#3 in front of the whole crew, and a file annotated *recovered to a peer institution.* Nobody has ever gone to look at which institution.

## The Crowns

The whole industry rests on perhaps two hundred people, and Ravenna holds most of them.

A waking trough does not run itself. Somebody has to stand at the console in a brass crown and speak the call into the slurry (*wake, sort yourselves, come to the edge*) and keep speaking it, and listen to what comes back, and know from the drift of the film and the sound of the paddle and the smell of the steam when a batch has Black in it and has started to feed on the red. Twelve troughs, four crowns to a shift, three shifts. Add the sieve hall's construct and the lock-call rigs on the refining floor and Site \#3 alone runs on dozens of trained minds, layered, each watching the others.

They are not made quickly. The Conclave spends the better part of twenty years on one, and the work eats them: an operator who has stood at a trough for a decade has drift, and drift is what ends the career rather than any decision. Most of a refinement crew does not see fifty. The order replaces them out of Freeport, at a rate that has never once been comfortable, and it has never troubled itself about the ones going the other way: the invalided, the failed, the passed-over, the quietly dismissed. They leave the fence and become Freeport's problem and then nobody's.

## Across the Water

Forty miles east, across Falter Sound, there is a second blight of comparable extent and comparable richness, and it has never been worked. The Conclave has been trying to buy [[Spero]] for two centuries and Buralia has been refusing for two centuries, politely, on a schedule. At Ravenna it is a standing joke: the good site, the one with no Verdani and no bloodthorn and no black wells, sitting there in the sun being nothing. Men who have spent nine years cutting a face they hate will say they can see it on a clear day, and cannot, and say it anyway.

There is a mission on that shore now. The order knows; a church buying ground in Buralia is not a secret and is not interesting. It is filed as what the Ecclesia says it is.

Two things follow from that, and neither has been noticed here yet. The first is that Ravenna is at the highest output in its history and is about to lose part of its market, because its largest buyer has spent a decade quietly arranging not to need it.

The second is that when mana starts appearing in Freeport at a third of Conclave rates, carrying Black, in volume (and it will), the order will not look across the water. It will look here, because this is the only place that mana has ever come from, and it will take the sites apart looking for the hole.

There is no hole. That is the interesting part. The controls at Ravenna are genuinely good and the accounting genuinely closes, and an investigation will spend a season proving it and come back with nothing, and be sent to do it again, because the alternative conclusion is that somebody else can refine mana and that is not a thing the order is able to think. It will be thought eventually, by somebody junior enough to say it out loud, and the moment it is thought there is exactly one place on the map it can point to. Ravenna's honesty is what will crack Spero open. It is going to take years and cost the careers of several people who did nothing at all.

**Features**
- Ground and growth getting wrong by degrees, with no line anywhere
- Refinery works: the face, the mill, the troughs, the sieve hall, the drying fields
- Site \#2's watchtowers standing over the road
- Site \#1 under ninety years of bloodthorn, fenced and unvisited
- Uncontrolled outcroppings out past the fence, unworkable and left alone
- Voids in the deep cut where the goo has flowed around something and not into it
- Blight wolves, and worse, larger every year
- Convoys of refined mana moving overland under escort, never by sea
- Verdani sign at the perimeters, and what follows it
- Workers with drift, and the flat humour they use about it
- Guild inspectors quarterly, writing in a book the order may not read
- Two locks, four weighings, and a body search at every gate
- Brass crowns, and how few heads there are to wear them
- The Buralian shore on the horizon, and the joke about it
- Sites that will not open a gate to another site's trouble
- The Duforq bridge, old Conclave stonework, the end of it

**Quest Starter**
> A quarry crew at Site \#3 cut a bench deeper than protocol allows and put a bar through into a void: a dry, sealed, pre-Fall space the goo has been flowing around for two hundred years without getting into. Racked inside, in rows, are several hundred slender rods of Unmade manufacture, undamaged. A Guild rep spent an hour alone with one and established the thing that will get people killed: it lit. In *his* hands. He has no Veil, has never had one, and will never have one, and he held the rod and made light with it and then warmed his tea.
>
> What he is holding is not a tool for making mages: no rod runs a waking trough, and everyone at Site \#3 who matters knows it within a day. It is a household object. It is the thing every ordinary person in the world used to own, and there are several hundred of them in a hole in the blight, and nobody has reported it yet.
>
> Every party that wants them wants a different world. The Conclave's monopoly is not really on refining, it is on the claim that mana needs a licensed man: it will pay anything for the stock and then bury it in the deepest vault it has. The [[Religion|Ecclesia]] preaches a caste order built on who was born clean, and a device that puts working mana in the hands of a Touched laundry girl is not a threat to its revenue but to its cosmology; it would want them destroyed and would not want to explain why. The Brotherhood does not need to understand a thing to sell it. And the crew who cut the bench are eleven men on shift wages who have grasped roughly what they are standing on, have not yet grasped that there is no version of this where they keep it, and are three days from telling their wives.
>
> None of the rods are charged. Nobody knows how they were meant to be.

**Second Quest Starter**
> A convoy out of Site \#3 has been taken. Not destroyed, taken, cleanly, by people who knew the schedule. The Conclave wants the cargo back and wants no one to learn it was ever missing. Recovering it means finding out who inside the site sold the timings, and the order has already decided what the answer will be.
