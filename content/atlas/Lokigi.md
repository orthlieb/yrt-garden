# Lokigi

*Town · Mososi · **Settled** · The road-head on the Bay; where the Materton haul becomes water · pop. ~800*

**Type:** Town · **Condition:** Well-kept · **Disposition:** Demanding
**First look:** Visited by large caravan or fleet · **Projects:** Trade · **Touchstones:** Adherence to authority
**Region:** Falter Bay · **Location:** Coast
**Location detail:** The western shore of Falter Bay, fifty miles south-west of Materton by road and thirty north of Typpe by water

**Exports:** Nothing of its own. Lokigi grows no food, cuts no timber, and makes nothing but chits. What leaves it is [[Materton]] grain and beans, [[Seka Lands|Seka]] salt in slabs, hides and dried curd, landed off wagons in the morning and gone on the evening ebb.

**Imports:** The same list running the other way, and it never stops: salt fish, iron, cordage, glass, cloth, lamp oil and Conclave seed, off the barges from [[Typpe]] and [[Freeport]], stacked in numbered sheds until a wagon is called for it.

Lokigi is a sum a clerk at [[Typpe]] did on paper about eighty years ago, and nobody has managed to argue with it since.

The road down from [[Materton]] runs fifty miles south-west along the dry margin of the [[Polvokovrita Gap]], and it is the worst road in Mososi. No water for two of the four days. Dry courses that fill from rain nobody standing in them has seen. Not one salaried official between the two ends of it. A loaded wagon makes it in four days with an escort and does not make it at all without one, and every family in Materton can name somebody who went out on it and did not come back.

From Lokigi it is thirty miles of flat enclosed shallow water to Typpe. A barge does that on one tide, with two men aboard, past a shore you can wade to.

So the road stops here. Everything Mososi's interior sends its capital, and everything the capital sends back, changes from wheels to hulls on this beach, and what that takes is a crane, nine sheds, a weigh house, and a board.

## The Board

Nothing is made in Lokigi and nothing is decided in it either. What happens here is a queue, and the queue is the whole of the town's public life.

A wagon is numbered at the gate, and from that moment the number is the only thing about it that counts: not the house, not the consignor, not whose son is driving. Numbers go up on the board outside the *prefekt*'s office, the board is revised at the noon and the evening bell, and a barge master loads in the order on the board and in no other order at all. The prefekt is salaried out of Typpe, rotated every three years, and forbidden to be flexible. That is word for word the instruction the *prefekt* at [[Termin]] works under, and it produces the opposite result. At Termin the process makes people wait. Here it makes things move, because the tide will not wait with them.

A barge goes on the ebb whether the board is finished or not. A wagon that came in an hour late holds its cargo a full turn. Every teamster on the Materton road knows this and drives the last stretch harder than the last stretch deserves, and the escort companies have priced that into their contracts.

## The Escorts

Four companies work the road and not one of them is Mososi in any sense the inspectorate is happy about.

They are engaged at the Materton end and paid off at this one, and their entire trade rests on the road being dangerous. It is dangerous. It is also worse in some years than in others, and the shipping clerks at Lokigi have noticed that the bad years arrive on a rhythm and have never once put that in a letter, because the alternative to hiring escorts is going without them.

The men take their pay at the wharf in coin and spend it inside a hundred yards. Mososi's answer to this is not a watch. It is a schedule. The drinking quarter is three streets at the south end, its hours are posted on a board of their own, and at the posted hour the doors shut. It works most nights. What it does not cover is understood to be somebody else's problem until the bell.

## What Does Not Come Through

Lokigi handles Mososi's own traffic and the traffic of one foreign shore, and the shore is [[Intersang]], forty miles across the water, which sends glass and sulphur and takes grain.

It handles nothing off the open sea, because nothing off the open sea can get here. Every hull on this water was built on this water and will be broken up on it. A Lokigi bargeman who has worked forty years on the Bay has never seen a vessel that could cross the [[Sea of Bees]], and if he wants to see one he goes to [[Freeport]] and stands on the quay like a visitor.

**Features**
- Wagons numbered at the gate, and the number chalked on the tailboard
- The board outside the prefekt's office, and the crowd in front of it at the bell
- Nine sheds in a numbered run, and a weigh house that never closes
- A crane on the beach, worked by four men and a donkey
- Barges going on the ebb with the board unfinished
- Escort captains counting out coin on a barrel head at the wharf
- Three streets at the south end with their hours posted like a tide table
- Teamsters asleep under their own wagons in the yard
- Dust off the Gap road on everything, including the fish
- Nothing growing anywhere inside the town, and no reason for it to

**Quest Starter**
> Three convoys have been hit on the last stretch of the Materton road this season, and all three were carrying for the same consignor, and all three were escorted by the company that got the next contract. The prefekt has the numbers, has done the arithmetic, and cannot act: the four companies are the road, and naming one of them means the other three set their price tomorrow. He wants somebody who is not on his payroll to go up the road and come back with something he can put in a letter.
