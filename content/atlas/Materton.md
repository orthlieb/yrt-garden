# Materton

*City · Mososi · **Settled** · The floating gardens; lake fish, ferries · pop. ~3,200*

**Type:** City · **Condition:** Prosperous · **Disposition:** Indifferent
**First look:** Built over or beside water · **Projects:** Farming · **Touchstones:** Rigid hierarchy
**Region:** Materton · **Location:** Lake
**Location detail:** A headland on the western shore of Lake Mater, with its beds out on the shallows

**Exports:** Food, in quantity and all year: beans, gourds, roots, greens and alliums off the young beds, grain off the old thick ones, salted lake fish, and seed stock that will not grow properly anywhere else. Materton feeds Mososi and sells the surplus to people who have no idea how it is produced.

**Imports:** Timber, iron, cloth, salt, and coal. Everything that is not food.

Materton is the mother town, and says so: the oldest continuously settled place on Lake Mater, older than Mososi's current dynasty, older than Freeport's canal, sitting on a low headland on the eastern shore where the ground behind is thin and stony and the water in front is not. Its walls are three ages of stonework stacked on each other and none of them are much use. Materton has not needed to defend itself in living memory and has quietly let the walls become the backs of houses.

The country around it cannot feed a city. Mososi's western reaches are the [[Seka Lands]] and the dust of the [[Polvokovrita Gap]], and the flats behind Materton are marginal and flood most springs. What feeds it is the lake. Out from the headland, covering some four square miles of shallow water, lie the **beds**: rafts of woven reed and lake weed, layered thick with mud dredged off the bottom, anchored on poles and staked in rows with channels between them wide enough to pole a punt down. They rise and fall with the water. They never flood and they never dry. The plants put their roots straight through into the lake and drink it.

They are absurdly productive, and everyone in Materton knows why without anyone writing it down. Somewhere under the shallows there is a Verdant source: a green mana reservoir the Unmade built and the Unmaking broke, seeping into the water ever since. Growth over the seep is unreasonable: three crops where two would be generous, beans that set in a fortnight, greens that have to be cut back rather than harvested. The strength falls off with distance, and the whole economy of the town is arranged around that gradient. A bed's worth is its position. Beds are inherited, mortgaged, and married over, and a bed near the middle of the seep is the kind of property families ruin themselves acquiring.

What it costs is paid slowly, and in public. The beds need re-mudding constantly, and the dredgers spend their working lives in the strongest water there is. It shows by the third generation. A green tinge comes up under the skin and deepens at the hands and forearms into something closer to bark than to callus. The limbs thicken. Movement slows, year on year, until a man of fifty crosses a room the way a much older man would, and is not in any pain about it. The face goes last and goes furthest, not sad, not blank exactly, just still, the way a tree is still. They stop wanting meat somewhere in their thirties and stop eating it altogether not long after. And they want the sun, badly: you will find them out on the beds at noon with their sleeves pushed up and their faces turned to it, not resting and not working, simply standing there for an hour at a time.

Materton calls them **Woodies**. It is not said cruelly. The bed-families are the wealthiest in the town, their beds are the ones everyone else covets, and nobody here needs telling what pays for that. They do not call themselves Verdani and would be insulted by the suggestion; whether the distinction survives another three generations is a question Materton does not ask aloud. The court at Typpe has known for a century, needs the food, and does not raise it.

That is Mososi's real strength and the reason a landlocked country with poor ground is a rival rather than a dependent. Nysis has the sea and the blight; Mososi has a lake nobody can blockade that produces more food than the country can eat. The Conclave's extraction monopoly is a grant of the Nysisian *chevayo* and stops at the border, and Mososi has never entertained the suggestion that it should extend. It has been asked. Politely, twice.

Everything on the lake also passes through here, because Materton is where the ferries are: north to [[Fluenti]], out to [[Sveba]], the long haul south-west toward the Typpe road. The ferrymen are a guild and an institution and a nuisance, and the arrangement by which they hold the crossing rights is the most contested thing in Mososi domestic politics. The court has twice tried to break it and twice given up.

**Features**
- Four square miles of garden beds on the water, staked in rows
- Punts working the channels between them at all hours
- Mud dredged from the bottom and spread by hand, endlessly
- Growth over the seep that is simply too fast
- Beds valued by position, and families ruined acquiring them
- Woodies out on the beds at noon, sleeves up, faces to the sun
- Small-boat fleets working the open shallows for pale lake fish
- Salting sheds, and the smell on the north wind
- Three ages of wall absorbed into houses
- Ferry quays, the guild that owns them, and the queue
- Sveba's lights out on the water at night

**Quest Starter**
> The seep has moved. Beds that carried three crops last year are carrying one, and a stretch of open water two miles north, owned by nobody, staked by nobody, has begun growing weed thick enough to walk on. Half the old families are ruined and half of Materton is about to be very rich, and the court at Typpe has sent someone to find out why before the fighting starts.
