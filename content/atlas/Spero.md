# Spero

*Remote · Buralia · Blight; worked off the books*

**Region:** Spero · **Location:** Waste
**Location detail:** The eastern shore of Falter Sound, roughly opposite Ravenna

Spero lies on the eastern shore of Falter Sound, in dry country roughly opposite [[Ravenna]]: a blight of comparable extent and, by the two surveys ever made of it, comparable richness. Buralia named it *Spero*, hope, in the years after the Unmaking, when it was expected to be the making of the empire. Nothing came of that. There is no river to move product on, no road that reaches it overland, and no fresh water within twenty miles to run a refinery with, and for two hundred years the name was a joke told with a particular face. That the two richest blights in the world face each other across forty miles of water, and that one of them made a Conclave and the other made nothing, is a fact Buralians raise more often than Nysisians enjoy.

Two hundred years of a blight left entirely alone is not the same thing as two hundred years of a blight under management, and the difference is the whole of Spero's reputation. Ravenna's outcroppings are surveyed, claimed, fenced and drawn down. Spero's simply continued. The scrub country deep in has gone through changes with no parallel in the west; the surveys describe standing formations rather than outcroppings, both surveys use the word *structure*, and the second one, which was better funded and got further in, spends four pages being careful about what it does not mean by that. Nobody goes out that far now. There is no need to.

## The Concession

The empire refused the [[The Conclave|Conclave]] (a Nysisian order arriving with a charter, a mine and a garrison) and has gone on refusing it, politely, every few years, and the refusal is the one piece of this that is a matter of public record. What the empire did not refuse was a church. A dozen years ago the [[Religion|Pura Ecclesia]] asked leave to put a mission in the dry interior, for the souls out there, and offered a rent for the ground it would stand on. The empire took the rent and accepted the distinction, which is thin, and which suits both parties to go on treating as real.

Behind the mission wall is a refinery. Nobody is *scavenging* at Spero, and nobody anywhere is: raw manite out of the ground is inert and worth nothing, and turning it into something a caster can use takes plant, water, trained hands and a tolerance for what goes wrong. That is the whole basis of the monopoly and the reason two centuries of resentment never produced a rival.

The Ecclesia is not a rival. The Ecclesia is the customer: the Conclave's largest buyer by a wide margin, paying monopoly rates on every seed burned in every rite, hospice, ward-lamp and reliquary it keeps, for two hundred years, to an order it regards as its own creature and its junior. It got tired of paying. It built the works with its own money, on its own ground, out of the reach of Nysisian law, and it staffed the works the same way.

## What It Cost to Make It Work

Building it was not the hard part. Making it produce was. The Ecclesia had the walls up and the troughs in and could not get a usable batch out of them for years, because a refinery is not a machine you buy but a process somebody has to know, and everybody who knows it works for the Conclave.

So they took one. When the Conclave quietly dismissed a senior Ravenna foreman (no charges, no public finding, a file annotated and closed) the Ecclesia had the news within days, because it keeps sources inside Conclave administration for exactly this. He was approached twice, in plain dress, and accepted at the second meeting. He was gone from Freeport inside a fortnight. His Conclave file records him as *recovered to a peer institution*, and nobody has ever gone to look.

He inherited a half-finished operation and was given whatever he asked for. It still took five years to get Spero producing to a standard anyone would pay for, and it has taken five more to admit that the one remaining fault will not be fixed. He has not left the site in a decade and has not needed to.

Which is the thing worth understanding about Spero: in ten years it has not sold a grain. Plant, rent, wages, carriage, bodies: all of it outward, none of it back, for a decade, on the word of a foreman nobody outside the order has laid eyes on since. The stock sits in the magazine and grows. Somewhere in Freeport there is a ledger nobody wants read aloud, and the men who signed it have had ten years to become frightened of it.

## The Workforce

The Ecclesia does not hire at Spero and could not if it wanted to. The nearest settlement is [[Alneta]], down the coast, and it is four hundred fen-folk who want nothing to do with the place; beyond that there is nobody, and no road home. It does not buy locally either: the Buralians out west trade in captives constantly and would sell, but a taking in that country is understood to end, and the tribes count their people back. The church needs labour that nobody is going to count.

It has some. It made some.

The [[Religion|Pura Ecclesia]] has for two generations taken Touched children out of Freeport's **Stews** as *oblates*: bought from destitute families with alms that are not called a price, given wholly and permanently, raised in the church's houses and taught that they are unclean and that a lifetime of unpaid service is the only road open to them. They staff the laundries and kitchens and hospices of every Ecclesia establishment in the city, and Freeport considers the arrangement charitable.

An oblate who runs, steals, argues doctrine where the others can hear, or whose beast-nature comes up too far to be shown to visitors is not turned out: a turned-out oblate is an indoctrinated witness standing on a public street. They are told there is a house in the east where the work is harder and the cleansing is complete. They are walked a day east of Freeport to a small bay nobody watches, put onto a Buralian hull by skiff, and that is the last of them. The harbour chains and the Grand Office are never involved; the coast is not the harbour, and nobody counts boats on an empty beach.

The hull is not imperial and has no business being anywhere near this. It belongs to the [[Weirden Fen]] trade: one of the shallow-draft coasters that run fen produce into Freeport and come back loaded with what will not grow in standing water. Fruit by the barrel, medicinals for the hospices, fungus, smoked eel, and alder piling, which does not rot underwater and which Freeport buys by the shipload because Freeport is built on a sinking isthmus. Inbound they are full. Outbound they were never full, and a fen skipper who has spent his life counting a return leg in ballast does not need the proposition explained twice.

So the run east is *backhaul*. That is the whole reason it is invisible: nobody built a slaving route, they hung a deck cargo on a trade that has been going both ways for longer than anyone can remember. The boat is shallow enough to work an open beach off skiffs, which a deep-draft merchantman could not do and which is why the bay east of the city is usable at all.

The crews find nothing remarkable in it. Slaving is lawful where they come from and they take the cargo for chattel of the Nysisian orders: *Conclave slaves*, in their phrase, the difference between the two orders not being visible from that distance. But a taking in their country runs its term and the man walks home at the end of it; that is what the word means where they are from. It has not occurred to them to ask what the term is here, and nobody has told the passengers they are not going to a mission.

Casualties at the works run well above [[Ravenna|Ravenna's]], and there are no inspectors, no guild, no grievance procedure and no ceiling on a replacement request. Which produces the quiet obscenity at the centre of this place, and it does not run the direction people assume. Spero does not consume what Freeport happens to discard. Spero *sets the discard rate*. A house that used to send one troublesome oblate east in a year now sends six, because a request came down and nobody at that house has ever been told why. The almoners in the Stews have been instructed to be less particular at intake. The definition of unmanageable has moved, and it moved because a refinery four hundred miles east needed bodies, and not one person in the chain between the beach and the bay believes they are doing anything but the church's work.

## What the Empire Knows

Not nothing. An empire that maintains a post on the approach and collects a rent every year for twelve years is not blind, and the question is worth asking precisely because the answer is not *yes* or *no*.

At the capital, Spero is a line: a mission concession in a worthless district, granted to a foreign church, rent paid punctually and in silver. Nobody there has been told anything else, and nobody there has asked, because the district produces nothing and questions about it cost more than they return.

In the provincial administration, somebody knows better. The rent has been renegotiated upward twice for reasons that were not written down. The post on the approach turns people back, which happens to serve the mission perfectly, and no one has ever explained why an imperial garrison is providing security for a foreign religious house at imperial expense. It is understood locally that there is more behind that wall than a mission, and it is understood just as clearly that nobody is to write it in a report, because a report creates an obligation, and the obligation would be either to extort a foreign order (which invites the Conclave and Nysis into a question the empire has spent two centuries keeping them out of) or to shut down the largest source of hard silver in a district that has never produced any.

But what they think they are tolerating is *mana*. The empire refused the Conclave on sovereignty, not morality; a church quietly working the blight is a violation of the same principle at a scale small enough to price, and pricing it is what they have done.

What nobody in the imperial administration knows is the labour: that the works is staffed by Nysisian subjects imported by sea and worked to death inside imperial territory by a foreign institution. That is not a moral problem for anyone concerned; it is a sovereignty problem, and a far worse one than the mana. The moment it is spoken aloud the empire stops being the landlord who charged rent and becomes the accomplice who was paid, and it has handed Nysis a grievance it cannot answer and did not need to give. The Buralians have been taking money for twelve years to look at the front of a building. They have never once asked what came ashore on the other side of it.

## One Crown

Everything Spero gets wrong comes from a single shortage, and it is not money.

A waking trough does not run itself. Somebody has to stand at the console in a brass crown and speak the call into the slurry (*wake, sort yourselves, come to the edge*) and go on speaking it, listening to what the trough answers, and know from the sound and the drift and the colour of the film when a batch has Black in it and is beginning to feed. At [[Ravenna]] there are twelve troughs and four crowned operators on every one of three shifts, and the sieve hall runs off a built amber construct the size of a wagon, and the refining floor has its own rigs speaking the lock-call into every cartridge. Dozens of trained minds, layered, watching each other.

Spero has one. It has always had one.

The order could buy the plant. It could copy the troughs, cast the crowns, and haul the water. What it could not buy was the twenty years the Conclave spends making somebody who can wear one, and it has never worked out how to get more of them. So there are crowns at Spero hanging on pegs with nobody to put them on, and the works runs at whatever fraction of itself one man can stand in front of. He cannot be at the troughs and the sieve hall at once. He cannot take three shifts. He cannot listen to twelve troughs, and he does not; he listens to two, and the others run to a schedule and are skimmed by men who were told what the film should look like.

That is where the Black gets in. Not through corruption or haste. Through the ordinary fact that nobody was listening. A trough left to a shift boss produces a batch that is *mostly* right, and mostly right compounds, and ten years of mostly right is a magazine full of product that no Conclave gate would pass. The output is also small. Far too small. Whatever the order thought it was buying, what it has is one man's working day, repeated for a decade.

The order is aware of all of this, and has concluded that the site does not need more money, more plant or more bodies. It needs heads that can wear a crown, and there is exactly one place in the world they exist.

Buying three or four would get the troughs running. It would not fix anything, because in ten years those men will be old or drifted or dead and the order will be back where it started. What would fix it is one person able to *train* crowns at Spero, and that is a different thing to go looking for, and a much slower one, and it would not merely leak the Conclave's monopoly. It would end it. Somebody is in Freeport now, taking as long as it takes.

## The Shipment

Spero has sold nothing in ten years. What is in the magazine now is the whole of it, and it is going west in a single hull.

It does not go out on a beach or over a border. It goes down to the water, then south the length of Falter Sound past [[Alneta]], and then in through the front of Freeport in daylight, because Freeport's harbour is barred by chains slung between the Grand Pylons and there is no unofficial way past them. Entry is bureaucratic or it does not happen.

Which is where the [[The Brotherhood|Brotherhood]] earns its cut. The church cannot write a night clerk's hand into the daily customs ledger at the Grand Office. The Brotherhood can. A ship stands off at the Fey Docks after midnight; its manifest goes up on the two o'clock runner; and somewhere between that runner and the morning the entry acquires a copper chit, a gate, an entry time, a flag code and an inspection grade of *cursory* that the ship's captain has done nothing in his career to deserve. The chains come down before noon and the cargo lands as grain and dry goods, duty assessed and paid in full, which is the part that makes it work: nothing about the entry is irregular except that it is false.

The captains for this run are chosen for having nothing to lose. The last one was down in the Grand Office's own book of listings as poor reputation, several prior infringements, and known associates in [[Piscis]], which is to say the Brotherhood put a marked man on the most sensitive cargo the church has ever moved, and bought him a clean entry rather than a quiet one.

The forgery is not about duty. It is about the inspection. A full rummage at the gate would find the taint in an hour. So the entry is written *cursory*, the barrels go up the dock like grain, and the Brotherhood is paid and is not told what is in them.

The decision to send it anyway was not made at the works. After ten years and that much money, *we will fix it in the next batch* had stopped being an argument anyone senior was willing to sit through. So the whole decade goes west in one hull, on one forged entry, and every barrel in it carries the fault the site has never once been equipped to solve.

**Features**
- Dry scrub giving way, further in, to something that is not scrub
- Standing formations rather than outcroppings, and still growing
- A mission wall far too long for the mission inside it
- Refining plant modelled on Conclave designs, newer and cruder than Ravenna's
- Every barrel of water on the place hauled in from twenty miles
- Contained tailings wells, and no inspector who has ever seen them
- Brass crowns on pegs, more of them than there are heads to wear them
- Troughs running to a schedule with nobody listening to them
- Work gangs who speak Freeport dockside and were promised a mission
- Children among them, and nobody at the works remarking on it
- An imperial post on the approach that turns back everyone it is meant to
- Imperial rent taken annually, for the ground and not for what stands on it
- Two surveys, one of them careful about its own vocabulary
- The name, and what people do with their faces when they say it

**Quest Starter**
> A lay brother off the Spero mission has reached Freeport and stopped sleeping. He is not asking anyone to shut the works down; he has understood that nobody with the power to do it wants to hear about it. What he wants is for somebody who is not of the church to go out there, get inside the wall, look at the wagons coming up the track, and come back able to say what they saw. He can put you on a mission cart. He cannot get you off one.
