# Pinna Mtns

*Remote · Nysis · Iron, coal, smelting; the district that eats people*

**Region:** Pinna Mtns · **Location:** Mine
**Location detail:** The spine of the region, Termin at its western foot and Collima at its eastern

The Pinna is the spine of the region: a long range running north-west to south-east with [[Termin]] at its western foot and [[Collima]] at its eastern, and everything of value in it is underground. What sits on the surface is the cost. The ore is smelted where it comes out, because moving rock is stupid and moving iron is not, so the workings are ringed by smelters and the smelters by tailings, and the tailings have made a blistered dead country of the valley floors: slag in terraces still warm a week after tipping, water the colour of rust with nothing living in it, ground that has grown nothing in decades. Nothing is farmed in the Pinna. Every loaf eaten here came up a road.

One thing does go back down it. The coal wagons that come east out of [[Termin]] would otherwise return empty, and for a decade now they have returned loaded instead: white quartz, calcite and a soft black ore picked out of the tailings by contract and carted to the glass sheds at [[Vitre]]. No smelterman can say what the third of those is for. It is waste rock; they are paid to be rid of it; and the arrangement is the only instance in the district's history of the Pinna being sold something it had already thrown away.

Two workings dominate. **The Empire Mine**, on the western road out of Collima toward Termin, is the larger and the older, and it is the reason Collima exists in the shape it does. **The Providence Mine** lies on the western road south of the Duforq bridge, close enough to [[Ravenna]] that its gangs are drawn from people with no better offer, and its output goes south under escort. Beyond those two are a hundred smaller claims (families, cooperatives, a dozen men on a seam they defend themselves) and a great many holes that nobody is working and nobody has mapped.

## The Ore, and the Accident In It

Pinna iron is harder and tougher than iron has any business being, and Pinna steel is the standard against which armour and weapons are priced in three countries. Everyone knows this. Nobody knows why.

The reason is manganese (the ore carries several times what any other deposit does) and manganese is not something anyone here has isolated, named, or would recognise in a dish. It simply comes out of the ground already in the iron and does its work unasked. What the district has instead of an explanation is **lore**: this seam is good, that one two hundred yards along is ordinary, the black-banded rock takes a temper and the brown does not. Every one of those rules is correct and every one of them was arrived at by ruining batches for a century until the pattern showed.

This matters more than it sounds. Pinna's whole position rests on a property nobody can describe, which means nobody can go looking for it anywhere else. The day somebody assays this ore properly and understands what they are reading, the Pinna stops being the only place in the world that makes iron like this. Nothing about that day is currently on anybody's calendar.

## The Work

It is hand work, and it is wet.

The upper seams were taken out generations ago and what is left goes down, which means water. Every working in the Pinna is a fight against water: adits driven long and level purely to drain a face, rag-and-chain pumps worked in shifts, horse gins turning on the surface all day to lift what the adits cannot. A mine that loses its pumping for four days loses the workings below the level, and there are galleries down there that were abandoned to flooding a lifetime ago and are still, on paper, somebody's property.

Rock that will not yield to a pick is taken by **fire-setting**: a fire built against the face and fed for hours, then the rock quenched and cracked and barred out while it is still too hot to lean on. It works, and it is how the hardest ground has always been won, and it drinks fuel and fills the workings with smoke that has to clear before anyone goes back in. Every man in the district can tell you the smell of air that is safe and the smell of air that is not, and a fair number of them learned it too late.

The smelters run on the streams. The water is dead (orange, sour, and useless for anything that drinks) but a dead stream turns a wheel exactly as well as a living one, and the wheels drive the blast. Furnaces run day and night because a blast furnace that is allowed to cool must be broken out and rebuilt, so the glow over the Pinna valleys does not go out, in any weather, in any season, and has not in living memory. Travellers on the Termin road at night navigate by it.

## The Gangs

Labour comes in three kinds and they work the same faces.

**Hired men** are skilled and paid properly: hewers, timbermen, furnace hands, the pump gangs. They can leave whenever they like. Most do not, because there is nowhere within four days' walk that pays half as well for anything they know how to do, and because the district has a way of making a man's whole life happen inside it: he is paid at the company office, he buys at the company store, and the difference between those two numbers is the reason he is still here at fifty.

**Pressed men** are here for debt. A debt in Nysis can be worked off at the Pinna, and the arithmetic of how fast is done by the company holding both the debt and the wage book. Some of them clear it. The ones who do not tend to stop mentioning it.

**Sentenced men** are here because a court sent them. Terms at the workings are the standard answer in Nysis to a wide range of offences, handed down at Freeport and in every town with a magistrate, and they are handed down freely because the mines always want bodies and everybody involved understands that they do. Nobody calls this a labour supply. It is called justice, it is legal, it is public, and the men who pronounce it sleep well. The Pinna is where Nysis puts the people it has decided not to think about any further, and it does so in daylight, in open court, with a clerk writing it down.

The three groups do not mix off shift and do not much mix on it. A hired man at [[Collima]] on his rest day will not be seen drinking with a gang. Everyone in this district can place everyone else within a sentence and a half.

## Fuel, and the Thing Nobody Says

Two fuels come in and they are not interchangeable, which is the whole basis of the district's economy and is set out in [[Pinna Mines]]. Coal west from [[Termin]] heats everything except the furnaces. Charcoal east from [[Collima]] does the smelting, because charcoal is nearly pure carbon and coal is not, and coal's sulphur makes the iron *red-short*: sound cold, brittle the instant it is worked hot, and an armourer can tell within three blows.

The district could survive losing the coal. It could not survive losing the charcoal, and the charcoal comes off a forest that is receding a little further every year to supply it. Everyone concerned understands the arithmetic. Nobody has anything to put in its place, and nobody is looking very hard, because the man who solves it ends Collima and knows it.

## What Is Down There

The Pinna has been mined for longer than Nysis has existed. The Unmade worked this range, and worked it in ways nobody now can read: square-section galleries cut through rock that should have taken a season and did not, level floors, drainage that still functions, and voids sealed and dry where two hundred years of water has gone around rather than in.

Small claims break into these more often than is reported, and they are not reported, because a claim that reports one loses it to the Empire Mine, to the Conclave, or to whichever of them gets a man there first. So the old workings are entered privately, by four men and a rope, and what comes out of them enters the district's economy without a docket and without a story attached.

**Features**
- Slag in terraces, cooling, and the heat coming off it days later
- Furnace glow over the valleys, in every weather, never out
- Water the colour of rust, turning wheels perfectly well
- Ore wagons on braked runners coming down the grades
- Horse gins turning on the surface all day, lifting water
- Fire-set faces, and the wait for the smoke to clear
- Hired, pressed and sentenced men on the same face, not mixing
- Company store prices, and the arithmetic that keeps a man at fifty
- Charcoal in from the east, coal in from the west, food in from everywhere
- Adits and shafts, worked and abandoned, unmarked and unfenced
- Square-section galleries that nobody in this century cut
- Old workings that go further in than anyone will admit

**Quest Starter**
> A seam in one of the small claims has broken through into a working nobody put there: cut stone, square section, dry, and going down. The cooperative that found it has not reported it, because reporting it means the Empire Mine or the Conclave takes it. They have four men and a rope and they want someone who has done this before. They have also, in the fortnight since they broke through, become extremely careful about who they drink with.
