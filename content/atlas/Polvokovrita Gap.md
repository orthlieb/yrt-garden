# Polvokovrita Gap

*Remote · Mososi · The dust-covered gap; the direct south-western road*

**Region:** Polvokovrita Gap · **Location:** Ravine
**Location detail:** A rift running forty miles south-west of Termin toward the Seka Lands

The name means *dust-covered*, and it is a description of the only route, not of the terrain around it. South-west of [[Termin]] the ground splits. A rift runs for some forty miles down toward the [[Seka Lands]]: walls of pale broken rock, in places a mile apart and in places close enough to throw a stone across, floored flat and level the whole way. The flat floor is what makes it a road. The reason the floor is flat is what makes it Remote.

It is filled with dust. Not soil, not sand: a fine pale grey particulate, ankle-deep in the open reaches and waist-deep in the hollows, that holds no moisture, packs under a boot and then releases, and hangs in the air for an hour after anything disturbs it. The Refiners' Guild has looked at it and will tell you, without ceremony, that it is degraded Gray: programmable matter from something the Unmaking broke, ground down over two centuries into a medium that is no longer doing anything but has not stopped being what it is. It is not poison. It is not inert either. Long exposure produces the same **drift** the refinery hands know: a change in how a person thinks and feels and mends, arriving too slowly to notice from inside.

Caravans use it anyway, because the alternative is three weeks around. They go fast, they go veiled, they do not camp in the hollows, and they do not go at all when the wind is in the wrong quarter: a Gap wind can put a moving wall of the stuff down the rift at the speed of a horse. Everyone who works the Gap knows a story about it. The stories are consistent, which is unusual, and the consistent part is that the dust arrives before the sound.

## What Lives In It

Nothing should. There is no water on the floor of the Gap and no possibility of any, and a surface animal that breathes forty miles of Gray particulate does not last a season. And yet the rift carries a complete food chain, three tiers deep, and the reason is that none of it lives on the floor. It lives in the walls, and it comes out.

A rift is a wound, and the rock either side of this one is broken to a great depth: fissures, undercuts, chimneys and collapsed voids running back into the country on both sides. Deep in that there is water: seeps, not springs, working slowly through the rock and never reaching daylight. Around the seeps is a lightless system of fungus, blind insects and the things that eat them, and it has been there far longer than the dust has.

**Trogs** work that system and are the foundation of everything above them. Warm-blooded burrowing reptiles, scaled, long-clawed, heavy through the tail, with a hide that carries a faint green-white luminescence, which is not decoration in a place with no light but the only illumination in the entire system. They tunnel constantly, they seal behind themselves, and the dust does not get into a trog run. They come to the surface at night and at the mouths, and that is where they stop being the top of anything.

**Wyverns** nest in the walls, high, in the undercuts and the chimney mouths, and hunt the rift floor the way a hawk hunts a hedgerow, except the hedgerow is forty miles long, a mile wide in places and flat, with nothing on it taller than a cart. It is the best hunting corridor in the region and they have it to themselves. These are the **iron** breed and they are the heaviest-plated wyverns anybody has recorded, with a hide that genuinely rings when it is struck, and the reason for that is in the next paragraph. They take trogs at the mouths, they take stray stock, and roughly once a year they take a person off the back of a moving caravan, which the caravan discovers at the next halt.

**Wyrms** are underneath. Massive, serpentine, wingless, short-legged and enormously long-lived, they lair in the deep voids, sleep for weeks at a stretch, and wake hungry. They have poor eyes by daylight standards and it does not matter: a wyrm hunts by **vibration**, and it can read the ground the way a person reads a page.

Which brings this whole ecology to a single point that everybody working the Gap has learned without ever being told the mechanism. Fine dust, forty miles of it, ankle-deep and level, is the finest medium for transmitting footfall that exists anywhere. A caravan crossing the Gap fast (which is the only sensible way to cross it) is a drum roll running the length of the rift floor, audible through the ground to something that is not listening with its ears. Going quickly is correct and it is also exactly what is being detected.

And the hollows. The reason nobody camps in the hollows is not the depth of the dust, or the bad air, or the difficulty of getting a cart out. It is that waist-deep dust will cover a wyrm completely, and does, and has. Nobody who works the route can tell you this in those words. What they can tell you is which hollows, and they are all agreed, and the list has not changed in thirty years.

## The Dust Goes Up

There is one further thing and the Refiners' Guild has written it down without publishing it. Degraded Gray in the ground does not stay in the ground. It goes into the seep water and into the fungus and into the blind things, and a trog that spends its life in that system carries more of it than the rock does; a wyvern that eats trogs for forty years carries more than the trogs; and a wyrm that eats both and lives for two centuries carries more than anything else in the region.

That is what the metallic sheen on a Gap wyvern actually is. Gray in the dermal layer, concentrated up three tiers of a food chain, doing what Gray does: binding to matter and making it structural. It is why the plating is heavier here than in the Tempest country, why a crossbow bolt skids off one, and why an old wyrm in the Gap is a genuinely difficult problem rather than merely a large animal.

It also means the caravan men and the animals are undergoing the same process by different routes. The men breathe it and get **drift**. The wyrms eat it and get armour. Nobody has ever remarked on the symmetry, because the two groups have never once had a conversation.

**Features**
- Pale rock walls, a mile apart or close enough to touch
- A floor flat and level for forty miles
- Grey dust ankle-deep, waist-deep in the hollows
- Air still hazy an hour after anything passes
- Veiled caravans moving fast and not stopping
- Hollows that nobody camps in, and the same list of them for thirty years
- Wind putting a wall of dust down the rift with no warning
- Things half-buried and preserved dry: carts, bones, gear
- No water, and no possibility of any
- Drift in anyone who works the route for a living
- Green-white glow at a fissure mouth after dark
- Wyvern nests high in the chimneys, and the whitewash below them
- A bolt skidding off plating that rings when it is struck
- Forty miles of level dust carrying every footfall to something

**Second Quest Starter**
> A Refiners' Guild man has worked out that the Gap is loading Gray into everything that lives in it, tier by tier, and wants to know how far up it has gone. He needs hide off a wyrm, and it has to come off a living one, because nobody has ever recovered a dead wyrm from the Gap: the dust covers a carcass inside a day and it is never found again. He has funding and a plan and has never set foot in the rift.
>
> The caravan men have refused at any price and have been polite about why, and the reason is not the wyrm. If the dust concentrates in a trog and a wyvern and a wyrm, it concentrates in a man, and drift on this route stops being exposure and becomes something that adds up over a working life. Every one of them has already done the arithmetic in his head. None of them wants the number from a man who will go home afterwards.

**Quest Starter**
> A caravan is overdue in the Gap and the wind has been wrong for four days. There is a window of perhaps a day and a half before it turns again. Whatever is standing out there in the dust has been standing there long enough that the question is no longer rescue, except that someone is paying as though it is.
