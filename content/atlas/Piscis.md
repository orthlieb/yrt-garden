# Piscis

*City · Nysis · **Settled** · Salt fish, salt, coastal freight · pop. ~2,400*

**Type:** City · **Condition:** Modest · **Disposition:** Wary
**First look:** Protected by stout walls · **Projects:** Hunting or fishing · **Touchstones:** Independence from authority
**Region:** Piscis · **Location:** Coast
**Location detail:** The north shore where Falter Sound widens into the Sea of Bees

**Exports:** Salt fish above all, split and salted and smoked and barrelled in brine, in quantities that feed abstinence days across three countries. Coarse salt off the pans north of the strand. Fish oil, glue, and meal. All of it goes out by sea.

**Imports:** Everything else, and all of it by ship: grain, timber, iron, cloth, cordage, canvas, wine. Freeport is four days down the Sound and is, in every practical sense, where Piscis buys its life. Much of it is landed at [[Ostrea]] first, where the water is deep enough, and carted sixty miles up the coast road.

Piscis stands on the northern shore where Falter Sound widens into the Sea of Bees, built on a shelf of grey rock above a harbour that was never deep enough. Deep-draft traders anchor out in the roads and wait (sometimes days, in a blow) while lighters work back and forth beneath them. Everything that enters or leaves Piscis is handled twice. The city has made a trade of that inconvenience: there are more porters here than fishermen, and the lighter crews hold a charter, a hall, and a long memory for grudges.

The fleet goes out before dawn, long open boats under tan sails, running for the banks where the cold water turns. What comes back is gutted, salted, smoked or brined inside the hour, because summer here is unforgiving and the drying racks along the Nether Strand run for the better part of a mile. The whole city smells of it. Visitors stop noticing on the second day. Elsewhere in Nysis they say you can tell a man of Piscis in the dark.

The fish goes where the fasts are. A third of the year is abstinence, nothing warm-blooded may be eaten, and salt fish is what most of the region actually puts on the table on those days: cheap, keeps, travels inland, and permitted. Permitted *if only just*: the bloodless rank above fish in the Ecclesia's grading, and Piscis has spent two centuries being told so by [[Ostrea]], which sends oysters and a certain tone along with them. The two cities need each other completely and have never much liked each other. Ostrea has the deep water and no hinterland; Piscis has the roads and a harbour it must lighter everything into; the sixty miles of coast road between them is the only overland link either has that does not touch the blight, and both of them would be poorer without it and would rather not say so.

What shapes Piscis is that it is not, in any useful sense, connected to its own country. Between the northern shore and the rest of Nysis lies [[Ravenna]], and the road that skirts it runs the blight's edge for eleven miles before it turns inland. The road exists. It is on maps. Almost nothing goes down it, because almost nothing is worth what the passage costs: a cargo has to be small, dense and enormously valuable before it is cheaper to armour a convoy and walk it past the blight than to put it on a ship and sail four days to Freeport. Such convoys happen perhaps twice a year and are remembered by name. Everything else goes by water.

So the northern shore is a pocket. Piscis and [[Ostrea]] have each other, sixty miles of coast road between them, and the sea; and they have Freeport, which is where their fish goes and where their grain and iron and cloth come from. Ostrea has the deep water and no hinterland worth the word; Piscis has the shallows and the lighters and must handle everything twice. Neither is sufficient and both know it, and two centuries of needing each other has produced a relationship of total dependence and constant irritation.

## The Wild Ground

The country between the northern shore and the blight is not empty, and it is not anybody's.

The **Verdani** are the ones you can reason with. They come out of the green country west and north, they are purposeful, and they are not interested in a fish town: what they want is at [[Ravenna]], where the extraction is eating ground they consider theirs. A Verdani party crossing near Piscis is usually going somewhere else. Usually.

The **Broken** are what the blight makes of people over time. Work gangs that walked off, camps that were never recovered, families three generations out there now who have never seen a wall. Drift taken past the point where it stops being a change in how a man thinks and becomes what he is. They are not a tribe and have no purpose beyond the immediate; they come in when the season is bad, take what is portable, and go. The Watch does not pursue them far, because pursuit means going into the ground that made them.

Everyone on this shore keeps a watch and everyone knows the bell. Raids are not a season and not a war. They are a fact of the place, like the weather off the Sound, and are planned around with the same weary competence.

## The Banks

And the trouble that actually costs Piscis money does not come from the land at all.

The banks where the cold water turns are the reason there is a city here, and Piscis is not the only fleet working them. The **[[Merrow|merrow]]** are out there (grey, scaled, long-limbed, hunched, with eyes like wet glass) and they fish the same ground with nets of their own, and have done so for longer than anybody has been keeping a record. They are not beasts and they are not raiders in any sense a Freeport magistrate would recognise. They are a people with a fishery, and it is the same fishery.

For most of two centuries this was survivable, because the banks were richer than either side could exhaust. That has stopped being true. The Ecclesia's calendar puts a third of the year on abstinence across three countries and Piscis is what feeds it, and the fleet has gone out further and stayed longer and taken more every decade to meet a demand set in a city four days away by people who have never seen a boat come in. The merrow have responded the way anyone would.

What that looks like from a deck is a net cut in deep water, a line fouled, and (three or four times in a bad year) a boat that does not come back and is found later with nothing wrong that anyone can point to. What it looks like to the merrow is not recorded, because nothing either side says is intelligible to the other; the merrow speak in clicks, grunts and whistles, and two hundred years of proximity have produced no interpreter, no treaty, and no meeting. This is a war over fish being fought by parties who have never once managed to say so.

Piscis is walled on the sea side and not on the land side, which every visitor notices and every visitor gets wrong. It is not for storm surge. Nineteen years ago the merrow came over it, and the town's answer since has been to keep the wall in repair, keep a watch on the water at night, and not discuss the matter with outsiders.

And underneath all of it is a question the Ecclesia has spent a great deal of energy not answering. If the merrow are beasts, then killing them is nothing, and Piscis has been doing nothing wrong for two centuries. If they are people, then a good portion of this coast's fishing fleet is guilty of murder over a disputed net. The Ecclesia grades every living thing that goes on a plate and has never once graded these. Both the fleet and the church prefer the silence, for different reasons, and neither has ever been pressed on it by anybody with the standing to press.

The *urbestro*'s customs house is not corrupt so much as tired. A captain who has worn out his welcome at Freeport's Dock Walk can generally find a berth here and a clerk who will write down what he is told, which is why *known associates in Piscis* is a line that appears in Freeport's own book of ship's listings, in the column where a night clerk decides how hard to look at a cargo. Nobody in Piscis has ever asked to have it removed. And the urbestro himself draws, like every Nysisian mayor, a share of the Conclave's annual rent for Ravenna: an income that arrives by ship from a chevayo he attends once a year at enormous inconvenience, paid for a blight that has done nothing for Piscis except cut it off from everywhere.

**Features**
- Deep-draft ships riding at anchor, waiting on lighters
- A mile of drying racks, and the smell that comes off them
- Tan sails standing out before first light
- Gull-clouds thick enough to throw a shadow
- Gutting sluices running open down the rock to the water
- Salt-cutters working the pans north of the strand
- Lighter crews who settle their own disputes
- A wall on the sea side of the city and none at all on the land side
- Merrow nets in deep water, and the fleet's own cut lines
- Customs clerks who ask the second question but not the third
- The coast road north to Ostrea, and wagons on it in all weather
- The haze over Ravenna behind everything, on a clear day
- The raid bell, and everyone knowing what it means

**Second Quest Starter**
> A skiff has come in off the banks with a merrow in it, alive, tangled in a Piscis net and hauled aboard by a crew who could not think what else to do and are now extremely frightened of what they have. It is injured, it is not fighting, and it has been making the same short sequence of sounds since dawn. The urbestro wants it dead and the matter closed before the Ecclesia's factor hears of it. Two of the crew have refused. Nobody in this city has ever had to decide what this is, and now somebody has about a day to.

**Quest Starter**
> One of the twice-a-year convoys is coming up the blight road, armoured and late, carrying something the Conclave has not described. Piscis has been asked to send men out to meet it. Not escorted in, *met*, eleven miles down a road nobody walks. The urbestro has agreed before asking anyone. Whatever is on those carts, somebody out in the wild ground already knows about it.
