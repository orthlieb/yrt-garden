# Overview

The Freeport Refiner's Guild is the workers' union representing labor at the Conclave's mana refinement sites: the men and women who work the face, the mill, the troughs, the sieve hall, the drying fields, and the refining floor. It is the only labor organization in Yrt with formal standing against the Conclave, and the only institution beyond the order itself that has the right to enter active refinement sites without invitation.

The Guild was founded after the disasters at Sites \#1 and \#2 made worker mortality politically intolerable. Its quarterly inspection regime is the regulatory mechanism that, in present-day Yrt, still catches what the Conclave's own oversight does not.

---

# Founding

The Guild was established in **142 SU**, in the wake of the disaster that destroyed Mana Refinement Site \#1.

## The Site \#1 disaster: The Year of the Black Rain (140 SU)

The destruction of Site \#1 was a single catastrophic event: a cascade containment failure that ended in the explosive release of a black well.

**Phase one: the trough.** A black-contaminated batch fed on red-charged dust and went into runaway hard-steam. The trough's luminous lining failed. The discharge ate through the floor of the refinement shed and worked its way downward.

**Phase two: the well.** In those days, black wells were sited *directly beneath the refinement shed* (the convenience-over-safety placement that pre-Guild Conclave doctrine permitted), and old-construction wells were **single-lined**: luminous interior shell only, no outer luminous layer. When the trough discharge reached the well, the contamination ate through the gray-concrete mantle from the inside out. Across the weeks that followed, the breach widened until the well released catastrophically.

**The Black Rain.** An explosive blowout from the wellhead. **Fourteen workers died immediately** in the blast. Corrosive material rained down for **hundreds of yards** across the site and the surrounding ground. Dozens more workers were sickened or injured by the falling material; some died painfully in the weeks and months that followed. The site's structures were torn open by the explosion; the surrounding earth was saturated with low-grade Black contamination that has never cleared.

The road past Site \#1 still runs to the Duforq bridge, but no one camps within the perimeter. The Verdant reclamation around the site's ruins (visible in the present day as Bloodthorn patches across the foundations) feeds on the residual Black Rain spread across the soil. The site remains uninhabitable to this day.

## Doctrine learned from the disaster

The Black Rain reshaped Conclave containment doctrine in three permanent ways:

1. **Black wells are sited far from active structures.** No longer beneath the refinement shed; no longer beneath any active building. A modern site's black wells are in a separate walled enclosure ("the black corral") at a remove from operations.
2. **Wells are double-lined.** Luminous on both sides of the gray-concrete shell. The inner lining contains the well's contents; the outer lining contains any contamination that breaches the inner lining. Single-lined wells from the pre-140 SU era have all been formally decommissioned (most by sealing in place; a few by re-construction).
3. **Black-contaminated discharge from a failed trough is now an evacuation event, not a containment event.** Standard protocol after the Black Rain calls for the affected section to be abandoned to the Black and the workforce evacuated to a safe perimeter. The old practice of attempting to seal contaminated sections with manual labor (picks and timbers, sandbags, masonry) is no longer permitted under Guild oversight.

The disaster was investigated by the Conclave's internal apparatus. The investigation concluded that the cascade was the foreseeable consequence of single-lined wells sited under active structures. **Site management was found culpable** for refusing to order an evacuation in the days between the trough failure and the well's eventual release: the workers had warned that the contamination was descending and that the well below was at risk, and management had ordered them to continue operations.

The Guild formed in the year after, with the explicit purpose of preventing the next such event from being explained away. Its founding charter declared three principles: **workers must be allowed to refuse unsafe work; sites must be subject to inspection by representatives of those who labor in them; and the institutions that profit from refinement bear responsibility for the conditions of refinement.**

Site \#2 had no comparable destruction event in its history. It has been continuously active since its founding (in the early decades after the Unmaking, as part of the Conclave's first refinement program) and is the elder of the active sites.

The Conclave initially opposed the Guild. Negotiations between the founding members, the Pura Ecclesia (which had quiet economic interest in worker stability), and the Brotherhood's predecessor mercantile guild (whose investments in the refinement economy were substantial) produced a formal compact under which the Conclave accepted the Guild's legal standing in exchange for the Guild's recognition of Conclave operational authority within the sites. The compact is called the **Concordat of Labor and Light** and is housed in the Freeport civic vault.

# Membership

The Guild represents:

- **Rank-and-file workers** at active refinement sites: quarriers, millers, trough operators (those without the brass crowns), sieve hall crew, rakers, packing crew, and the various adjacent labor positions.
- **Brass-crown operators**: Conclave-trained workers who speak to the dust in the troughs. These workers occupy a complicated position: technically Conclave-affiliated by virtue of their training, but workers in practice, and the Guild has fought (successfully) for their inclusion.
- **Under-foremen**: shift-level supervisors who came up through the ranks. Brusc Halvert (third shift, Site \#3) and Jaggar (second shift, Site \#3) are both Guild members.

The Guild explicitly does *not* represent:

- **Site leaders**: full foremen with administrative responsibility for the site. These are Conclave appointees; they're management. A site leader is not a Guild member, and his relationship with the Guild's onsite representatives is institutionally adversarial: the man who sets the shift is the man the complaints are about.

Membership is automatic for rank-and-file hires and dues-funded. Senior members elect regional representatives who in turn elect Guild leadership in Freeport.

# Structure

## Onsite representatives

Every active refinement site has one Guild representative drawn from the site's senior under-foremen. The representative's role:

- Collect worker complaints and safety reports
- Maintain the site's casualty and incident log (a parallel record to the Conclave's official log; the Guild's version is often more honest)
- Receive the quarterly Guild inspector and walk them through current operating conditions
- Funnel formal grievances through the Guild's institutional channel, which escalates to the Conclave Prefect's office through the Concordat-defined route

The representative is *not* a manager of complaints: they pass them up the chain. They do not have the authority to resolve disputes or alter operations. Their role is informational and institutional.

The position carries some hazard. A representative who is too vocal can find themselves moved to less favorable shifts, denied promotion, or quietly transferred to harder sites. The Guild's leadership protects representatives where it can, but the Conclave's power within its own sites is real, and a determined site leader can make a representative's life difficult without ever violating the Concordat in writing.

At Site \#3 the onsite representative is **Jaggar**, the second-shift under-foreman. Jaggar handles worker complaints with appropriate professionalism. He funnels them through the Guild's escalation path, and does not editorialize. The complaints accumulate.

## Quarterly inspectors

The Guild maintains a small cadre of professional inspectors (usually retired senior workers or former under-foremen) who travel between active sites on a rotating schedule. Each active site receives one inspection per quarter.

Inspection scope:

- Review of the site's casualty and incident logs (both Conclave and Guild versions)
- Walk-through of all operational areas during one full shift
- Interviews with workers (conducted privately, without site leadership present)
- Examination of equipment maintenance records
- Sampling of the air, the dust, and the field plots where worker exposure is highest
- Confidential conversation with the onsite Guild representative

Inspectors file two reports: one to the Guild leadership in Freeport, one to the site's Conclave overseer. The Guild leadership reviews patterns across sites and over time. The Conclave is officially required to respond to inspection findings within forty days; in practice the response time varies considerably depending on the political weight of the findings.

## Leadership

The Guild's leadership is small: a Master of the Guild (elected from regional representatives, term of seven years), a council of seven senior members representing the major active sites, and a small Freeport-based staff handling correspondence, archive maintenance, and the formal escalation pipeline.

# Relationship with the Conclave

Semi-official partner, legally protected, frequently antagonistic.

The Conclave cannot operate the refinement sites without the workers. The Guild ensures the workers organize. The two institutions need each other and resent each other in roughly equal measure. The Concordat of Labor and Light specifies the formal interface; the actual day-to-day relationship is a series of negotiations, accommodations, small refusals, and quiet escalations.

A few permanent points of tension:

- **The Conclave wants production targets met.** The Guild wants the targets adjusted for worker mortality. Site leaders, caught between, often push hard and rationalize the casualties.
- **The Conclave wants to handle ethical violations internally.** The Guild wants public accountability. The Concordat splits the difference: investigations are joint, dispositions are Conclave-led, public proceedings are rare.
- **The Conclave maintains formal training of brass-crown operators.** The Guild represents the operators day-to-day. Operators have effectively two employers and two loyalties.
- **The Conclave routinely understaffs sites to manage costs.** The Guild routinely files complaints about understaffing as a safety issue. The complaints accumulate.

The two institutions have not been at open conflict in any of the present generation's memory. But the friction is constant.


# Worldbuilding Notes

- **The Guild's archives in Freeport** are housed in a modest building in the Shy Side district. They contain decades of inspection reports, complaint files, casualty logs, and the Guild's correspondence with the Conclave Prefect's office, including the file on every site leader the Guild has ever forced the Conclave to recall. The archives are open to anyone who learns how to ask.
- **The Guild's leadership is generally honest, occasionally heroic, and institutionally cautious.** They are the moral conscience the Conclave can't be because they are not invested in protecting the order's authority. But they also work *with* the Conclave under the Concordat, and they do not risk the Concordat lightly.
- **The Brotherhood's interest in the Guild is complicated.** The Brotherhood profits from refinement-economy commerce, and major disruption at the sites is bad for business. The Brotherhood quietly supports the Guild's stability while exploiting its formal weakness wherever convenient.
- **The Pura Ecclesia's interest is theological.** Workers at refinement sites are disproportionately Touched. The Ecclesia's official position on labor is that the Touched should be available for whatever work the Pure assign them. The Ecclesia officially opposes the Guild's representation of Touched workers, and the Guild officially does not represent Touched workers as a class, but in practice the Guild protects them alongside everyone else, and the Ecclesia tolerates this in exchange for not being directly challenged.
