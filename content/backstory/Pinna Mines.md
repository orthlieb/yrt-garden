title: Pinna Mines
---

High in iron deposits, the Pinna mountains have been a rich source of the metal for many decades. Higher than normal manganese content has ensured that it is harder and stronger than most other iron deposits making it excellent for armor and weapons.

Most of the ore is refined in smelters on site with the tailings leaving a blistered landscape behind with little food and foul water. There are caravans of traders that bring in foodstuffs, coal from Termin, charcoal from Collima, and work gangs (pressed and/or hard labor).
## The Two Great Mines

Most of the Pinna range is worked by small operations — a family, a cooperative, a dozen men on a claim they defend themselves. Two workings dwarf the rest.

**The Empire Mine** sits on the western road out of Collima, on the way to Termin. It is the mine Collima exists to feed: charcoal goes west to its smelters, ore and pig iron come east into the town, and the traffic on that road sets the rhythm of Collima's week.

**The Providence Mine** lies on the western road south of the Duforq bridge, close enough to Ravenna that its work gangs are drawn from people who could not get hired anywhere better. Its output moves south along the blight road under escort. What Providence pays in wages it saves in the quality of the lives it spends.

See [[Pinna Mtns]] for the range itself.

## The Two Fuels

The workings burn charcoal and coal both, and the distinction between them is the whole reason Pinna steel is worth what it is.

**Charcoal smelts.** It comes east from [[Collima]], carted thirty-odd miles at considerable expense, and nothing else will do the job. Charcoal is very nearly pure carbon; coal is not, and the sulphur in it passes into the iron and makes the metal *red-short* — sound when cold, brittle the moment it is worked hot. An armourer can tell within three blows. Pinna's reputation rests on two things: the manganese that is in the ore already, and the charcoal that keeps sulphur out of it. Take the charcoal away and the manganese is not enough.

**Coal does everything else.** It comes west from [[Termin]], it is cheap, filthy and inexhaustible, and it is used for every purpose in the district except the furnaces themselves — roasting ore, burning lime, reheating stock at the forge, drying, and keeping the sheds and barracks above freezing through a Pinna winter. A mine that runs out of coal is uncomfortable. A mine that runs out of charcoal stops making iron.

The awkwardness is that charcoal is a poor way to spend a forest. Converting wood to charcoal throws away better than half the wood's heat before any of it reaches a furnace, and charcoal is so light for its bulk that hauling it costs more per unit of heat than hauling coal. Collima burns an enormous quantity of standing timber to keep the Pinna in fuel, and the Nebula recedes a little further every year to pay for it. Everyone concerned understands the arithmetic. Nobody has anything to put in its place.

## Composition of Ores
- **Hematite (Fe₂O₃):** ~50–70%  
    The dominant mineral in high-grade iron ore deposits, providing most of the iron content.
- **Magnetite (Fe₃O₄):** ~20–35%  
    Highly magnetic; often comprises a large portion in some deposits, especially with higher iron content.
- **Goethite / Limonite (FeO(OH)/FeOₓ(OH)ᵧ- nH₂O):** ~5–20%  
    Common in weathered and lower-grade ores.
- **Siderite (FeCO₃):** <5%  
    More common in specific deposits but typically a minor component.
### Gangue Minerals 
- **Quartz (SiO₂):** ~10–30%  
    Frequently forms bands or matrix in the ore, especially in banded iron formations.
- **Calcite (CaCO₃):** ~2–10%  
    Present in certain types of iron ore, especially those near limestone or in weathered zones.
- **Chamosite/Other Silicates:** <5%  
    Minor or accessory minerals depending on geology.
### Impurity Elements
Rough Ranges, by weight in total ore
- **Manganese:** <1–4%
- **Phosphorus, Sulfur, Alumina, Lime, Magnesia:** each usually <1%

**Typical high-grade iron ore:**
- Total iron oxides (hematite + magnetite): **60–70%**
- Gangue minerals (quartz, calcite, silicates): **15–30%**
- Minor and trace elements (impurities): **≤5%**
## Occasionally extracted/used minerals:

- **Manganese:** Sometimes present in iron ores and incidentally included in medieval iron, which could affect the steel's properties. However, manganese was not deliberately extracted and separated—the technology to isolate it did not exist in the Middle Ages.
- **Siderite:** This iron carbonate was sometimes smelted, but its carbon dioxide content made it less attractive compared to hematite and magnetite.
- **Lime (Calcite):** Used later as a flux in the blast furnace era, but mostly not extracted as a separate product—any calcite was just part of the flux and not used otherwise.
### Not extracted:

- **Quartz (Silica):** Ended up in slag, not utilized for any other purpose in medieval metallurgy.
- **Chamosite, Alumina, Magnesia, Phosphorus, Sulfur:** Present as impurities or slag components; not purposefully extracted or used.
