- Wings radiate faint heat; frost and snow melt in a radius when it lands
- Eyes burning amber-red, pupils slit-vertical, visible from altitude
- Droppings are ash-grey and still warm when they hit the ground

## Drives
- Hunt from thermals above Crimson-saturated terrain
- Defend nesting territory without exception
- Roost on warm outcroppings where Crimson mana seeps closest to the surface

## Tactics
- Circle high until a target is isolated and exposed on open ground
- Dive at terminal velocity; release stored thermal charge on contact — no flames, instantaneous heat transfer at point of impact
- Disengage and climb back into thermals between passes; rarely closes to sustained melee
- A mated pair staggers attacks to deny the target recovery time

## Description
Ember Kites are what happens when a raptor population nests for many generations at the edge of a Crimson blight zone. Their feathers absorb and retain heat through a biology that no ordinary raptor shares: the tissue throughout their flight musculature is threaded with Crimson-saturated capillaries that act as a living heat battery. A kite basking on a warm outcrop is charging. A kite diving is discharging.

The thermal release on impact is not fire. There are no flames, no ignition source, no visible effect until the moment of contact — at which point the stored heat transfers instantaneously into the target at a level sufficient to cause serious burns and ignite loose cloth or dry material. The bird itself is unharmed.

Ember Kites are territorial and predictable in range. A bonded pair will hold a stretch of thermal-rich cliff or ridge for years and will attack anything that enters their territory without exception, regardless of size. Travellers who know what to look for — the rust-orange silhouettes circling high thermals, the absence of other birds in an area, the shimmer of heat distortion near certain outcroppings — can usually route around them.

Conclave surveyors treat Ember Kite sightings as a reliable early indicator of Crimson contamination. The birds do not cause the blight; they only live where it already exists.
