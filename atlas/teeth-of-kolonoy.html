Teeth of Kolonoy

Remote · Mososi · Hoodoo country; wind, disorientation, old work

Region: Teeth of Kolonoy · Location: Ridge Location detail: Far to the south-west, out past the dry margin of the Seka Lands

Far to the south-west, out past the dry margin of the Seka Lands, the ground breaks into hoodoos. Several hundred of them stand across some eleven miles of pale badland: columns of soft banded rock, forty to two hundred feet, each one carrying a cap of harder stone that has sheltered the pillar beneath it while everything around eroded away. The banding runs in courses of cream and rust and grey, so the whole field is striped horizontally and the stripes line up across every column, which is the detail that undoes people. From a distance, along the skyline, the line of them reads as a jawbone lying on its side. The name is Mososi and the Kolonoy part is not a Mososi word and nobody can say whose it was.

Nothing gates them and nothing has to. The Teeth are not a wall. You can walk in anywhere, between any two columns, and that is the problem. Inside the field there are no sightlines longer than a hundred yards, every column looks like every other column, the banding gives you a false horizon that is not level, and the wind is loud enough and confused enough that sound arrives from the wrong bearing. People go in and circle. The Seka clans do not enter the field at all; they read it from outside, off the sound, because certain columns at certain wind strengths give a sustained tone, and the clans have names for the individual notes and take weather from them.

What is on top of them is older than the name. Cut steps go up the outside of four columns, worn but unmistakably cut. The tallest carries a levelled platform with the footings of something that was taken away rather than fallen. And in the undercuts, on the sheltered undersides where two centuries of weather has not reached, there are worked surfaces: dressed, tooled, not natural and not recent. Nothing lives up there now. People go up and come back down. The Teeth have no reputation at all for taking anyone. In a country where every other empty place has a story attached, this one has none.

The Voices

The tone is not mysticism and the Seka have never claimed it was. Soft rock erodes fastest where it is already hollow, so a good number of the columns are undercut at the neck and cavitied inside, and a cavity with a mouth of the right size does what a jug does when you blow across it: above a certain wind speed it speaks, at a pitch fixed by its volume, and it goes on speaking until the wind drops.

Each sounding column therefore has one note and only one note, it never varies, and it either sounds or it does not. That is what makes the field readable. A herder standing outside it hears which notes are speaking and knows the wind is above the threshold for those and below the threshold for the others, and hears which side they are arriving from, and knows from a lifetime of the combination what is coming across the Seka in the next day. The notes are named. Some of the names are clan property and are not given to strangers.

It is a genuine instrument and it is being lost. A hoodoo stands until its capstone slips, and then the soft pillar underneath goes quickly (a few columns a decade, more in a wet year) and when a sounding column falls, its note is gone and does not come back. The Seka have names for notes that no longer sound and teach those names anyway, so that a child will know what is missing from a chord their grandmother could hear.

The Four Columns

Steps go up four of them, cut into the outside, worn round but unmistakably worked.

They are not the four tallest and they are not clustered. They are spread the length of the field, and from the top of each one you can see the top of the next, and from the two on the ends you can see a long way out past the Teeth entirely: north-east toward the Seka, and south-west toward country nobody in Mososi has mapped. Anyone who has climbed all four has noticed this. Nobody has done anything with the observation, because there is nothing at either end to look at now.

The platform on the tallest is levelled, dressed, and drained, with sockets cut for footings. The sockets are clean. Whatever stood in them was unbolted and taken away, not broken off, not thrown down, not left to fall. In the undercuts below, where two centuries of weather has never reached, the tooled surfaces are as crisp as the day they were cut.

That is the genuinely strange thing about this place and it has nothing to do with the wind. Everything else the Unmade left in this region was broken while it was still running: Site One, the Dronitay, the ward on the Malperm, the works under the blight. The Teeth were decommissioned: shut down in an orderly fashion, by people who had time, who brought tools, and who thought it mattered that the thing be removed rather than abandoned. Nobody has ever offered a reason why a structure worth putting on four columns across eleven miles was also worth taking down neatly afterwards, and the absence of a reason is the whole of the site’s reputation among the few people who have thought about it at all.

Why Nothing Is There

There is no water in the Teeth, no soil worth the word, and almost nothing growing. There is therefore nothing to eat, and nothing that eats things, and no reason for anything to be there, which is the entire explanation for the field’s blameless reputation and is not a comfort to anybody.

The Seka are quite clear about why it bothers them. In their country empty ground means used-up ground: a well gone, a season overgrazed, a valley worked out. Emptiness is a thing that happens to a place, and it happens for a reason, and the reason is usually recent. The Teeth are empty in a way that has no cause anybody can point at, and the clans would genuinely rather the field had a reputation for taking people, because a place that takes people is a place you can understand.

Features

  • Several hundred banded columns across eleven miles of pale badland
  • Hard capstones perched on soft pillars, each one improbable
  • Horizontal banding that lines up across every column and lies about level
  • No sightline longer than a hundred yards once you are inside
  • Wind that is constant, loud, and arrives from the wrong direction
  • A sustained tone off certain columns at certain wind strengths, one note each
  • Named notes, including names for notes that no longer sound
  • A column down since last year, and the fresh pale scar of it
  • The whole line reading as a jawbone from the skyline
  • Cut steps up the outside of four of them
  • A levelled platform on the tallest, sockets cut clean and empty
  • Line of sight from each stepped column to the next, the whole length of the field
  • Tooled surfaces in the undercuts, sheltered from two centuries of weather
  • Nothing dangerous, and everyone uneasy about that

Quest Starter

A Seka herder has been reading the Teeth by ear her whole life and says a note has changed: one column now sounds where it never used to, and it has been sounding for a month. Something up there is different. She will not go in to find out and she does not think you should either, which is not the same as saying she will not point you at it.
