Malperm Mtns

Remote · Nysis · Forbidden ground; nothing extracted

Region: Malperm Mtns · Location: Mountain Location detail: The high range north of the Profunda, above the treeline

Malperm is a contraction of the old word for forbidden, and unlike most names in this region it is not poetic. The range stands over the Profunda Forest in a wall of grey rock and permanent snow, and there is iron in it, and there is timber on its lower slopes, and none of it is worked. Nysis does not forbid it. There is no ordinance, no posted edict, no patrol. The range forbids itself, and it has been doing so consistently for longer than the current arrangement of countries has existed.

What happens is documented and boring and absolute. Above a certain height (which varies with the valley, which people have mapped, which does not move) travellers stop. Not violently. They arrive at a place where continuing becomes a thing they find they are not doing, turn around without deciding to, and walk back down, and when questioned afterwards give accounts that are lucid, unembarrassed, and completely unable to explain the decision. The line is the same for everyone. It has been the same for two hundred years. Cairns mark it in a dozen valleys, built by people who came back.

The Conclave’s position, formed after three expeditions and one Grand Thesis that was not accepted, is that the Malperm carries a standing Luminous ward at a scale nobody now can build or match, laid down before the Unmaking around something the Unmade thought worth warding, and still executing. This is the least alarming available explanation and the order prefers it. It also means that whatever the ward was set to protect has been unattended for two hundred and thirty years, behind a fence that nobody has found the edge of.

The Band

Between the treeline and the line there is a band of country perhaps four thousand feet deep, and it is not empty.

It should be. It is barren for most of its width: scree, wind-scoured rock, snow that does not clear until midsummer and comes back early. But threaded through it are valleys that are nothing like that: deep, sheltered, south-facing, and green in a way that reads as an error at that altitude. Grass to the knee in a place where there should be lichen. Berry scrub carrying fruit the size of a thumb-end. Trees at a height where trees do not grow, and growing fast.

The reason is flow surviving in the rock and coming up in those valleys and nowhere else, and it is not one colour. There is Verdant in it, which is what puts grass at nine thousand feet. There is Azure in it too, which is the colour that works on a body without changing what kind of body it is. Neither is worth an extraction site: both are diffuse, bound in living ground rather than pooled in outcrop, and the Conclave surveyed the range once and wrote it off. What the Verdant is worth is food. A dozen valleys in a hostile range produce like farmland, forever, without being farmed.

What the Azure is worth is the rest of this entry.

That is the whole reason there are people up here at all.

The Giants

They are a people, not a monster and not an affliction. The grass in those valleys is Verdant work. The giants are not, and the difference is worth being exact about, because both colours reach a living body and they do different things to it. Verdant turns an animal toward the plant: Materton’s Woodies go green under the skin, thicken, slow, stop wanting meat and start wanting sun. Nothing of the kind has ever happened up here. Azure is the other road, and it does not change what a thing is. It makes more of it.

So the Azure in that water has been reaching these families for longer than anybody has been counting, and what it made them is large: a grown one stands a head and a half over a tall man and is built in proportion, heavy in the bone, deep through the chest. The cold suits them; a big body holds its heat, and a Malperm winter kills ordinary men who are doing everything right.

Everything else about them follows from what that costs to run. A giant eats three times what a man eats and cannot stop. So there are no towns, no gatherings, and nothing anybody would call a tribe: the country cannot carry a crowd. There are households, one to a valley or two to a large one, and a household is not a family so much as a claim on a quantity of growing ground. Valleys are inherited, exactly and jealously, and a valley that will not feed the people on it is the only real crisis this culture has. They are unhurried, they are formidable, and they are perpetually four bad seasons from disaster.

They do not raid for plunder. They come down when a valley fails (frost in the wrong week, a slip that takes the good ground, a spring that moves) and what they come down for is food, and they take it from wherever it is stacked, which is a town. This is famine behaviour and it is why it arrives without warning and stops without negotiation. The last descent anybody kept a record of was nineteen years ago, out of a valley that had lost a growing season to a frost in the wrong week, and it reached Fluenti’s outlying holdings and went back up within four days. Fluenti remembers it as a raid. It was a household that had run out of food and then stopped being hungry.

They are, in every account by anyone who has actually spoken with one, entirely reasonable people. Nobody in Nysis has arranged to speak with one on purpose.

The Ones Who Have Always Been Here

The giants have no quarrel with the Verdani and this is the one relationship in the Verdani’s world that simply works, for a reason more mechanical than either party has ever put into words. They want different colours off the same ground. Verdani go where Verdant flow is, and these valleys have it; the giants are an Azure people who need a valley to grow and have never gone the green way themselves. Neither cuts the flow, extracts it, or sells it. They eat what grows and the ground keeps growing. There is nothing to fight about, and a Verdani party wintering in a Malperm valley by arrangement is an ordinary occurrence that nobody in Freeport would believe.

And the giants know about the line, and have known about it for a great deal longer than Nysis. They have a word for the country above it. Rendered into Nyssian by people who did not entirely understand what they were being told, it comes out as something between the held and the kept, and the ambiguity, whether the thing up there is being held back or being kept safe, is present in the original and is not an accident of translation. They do not cross. They have never crossed. When asked why not, the usual answer is a look, and the observation that the questioner did not cross either.

Features

  • A wall of grey rock and permanent snow above the treeline
  • Good timber and visible ore, entirely unworked
  • The line: mapped, unmarked on the ground, identical for everyone
  • Return cairns built at the line in a dozen valleys
  • People coming back down calm and unable to say why
  • No animals above the line either, which is worse
  • Old survey stakes on the approaches, Conclave-cut, abandoned
  • Sheltered valleys carrying grass to the knee at nine thousand feet
  • Berry scrub in fruit where there should be lichen
  • Animals a size up from their own kind, in valleys that grow like farmland
  • One household to a valley, and the valley boundaries exact
  • Doorways, lintels and steps built to the wrong scale
  • Three expeditions’ worth of gear left where it was set down

Quest Starter

Someone has crossed the line. A Conclave party tracked her to it, watched her walk through, and could not follow. She has been in there for nine days. Whatever she is (and the order has a word for it that it will not use in front of you) she is the first, and both the Conclave and the Verdani want to be the ones standing there when she comes out.

Second Quest Starter

A valley three days above the treeline lost its spring in the autumn and the household on it will not last the winter. They have sent someone down, which has not happened in anybody’s memory, and which cost them something to decide: to ask whether grain can be bought, and with what, and by whom. She is waiting outside Fluenti because she will not fit through the gate, and the town has about four days to work out whether this is a negotiation or the last warning it will get.
